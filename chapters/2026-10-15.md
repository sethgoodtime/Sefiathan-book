# **Day 287 — October 15, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his fifteenth morning of October and the reading landed like permission to stop performing certainty: Stop lying to yourself. The story you keep telling isn't protecting you—it's keeping you small. You know what's true. You've known for a while. The elaborate justifications you're constructing aren't wisdom, they're avoidance. Today, name the thing you've been pretending not to see.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle with uncomfortable accuracy that made his stomach tighten. The past few days had been about opening—learning that his beautiful heart would bring beautiful things if he stopped hiding it behind walls, recognizing that past hurts didn't justify complete concealment, letting Korrin's genuine admiration surprise him instead of immediately deflecting it. He'd been practicing showing his warmth again. Letting his capacity for care be visible. Trusting that his beautiful heart wasn't weakness but gift.

But underneath all that opening had been a story he kept telling himself. A careful narrative about why certain things were the way they were. Why certain patterns kept repeating. Why certain people remained in his life exactly as they were. And the reading cut through all those elaborate justifications with brutal simplicity. Stop lying to yourself. You know what's true. You've known for a while.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling something he'd been avoiding pressing against the edges of his awareness. The story you keep telling isn't protecting you—it's keeping you small. What story? He knew immediately. The story about his family. The careful narrative he'd constructed about why his father would never see him, why his mother's validation always came with conditions, why going home would somehow be different if he just transformed enough first. He'd been telling himself that the problem was him. That if he just grew more, understood better, became stronger—then they would finally see him clearly. Then everything would change.

But that was a lie. And he'd known it was a lie for months now. Maybe longer. The elaborate justifications you're constructing aren't wisdom, they're avoidance. All his careful analysis of family dynamics, all his understanding of why they were the way they were, all his compassionate recognition of their limitations—it was all true. But it was also avoidance. A way to not name the actual truth he'd been pretending not to see.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she took one look at his face and set down her spoon.

"What did the reading say?"

"That I need to stop lying to myself. That the story I keep telling isn't protecting me, it's keeping me small. That I know what's true and I've known for a while." His voice came out quieter than he intended. "That the elaborate justifications I'm constructing aren't wisdom, they're avoidance."

Erenne poured tea and sat down across from him. "What's the lie?"

Sefiathan felt his throat tighten. "That going home will be different. That if I just transform enough, grow enough, become strong enough—then my family will see me clearly. Then my father will think I'm enough. Then everything will change." He stopped, feeling the truth pressing up through the careful story he'd built around it. "But that's not true. I've been lying to myself about what's waiting for me in Millhaven."

"And what's the truth?"

He sat with the question, feeling all his elaborate justifications trying to rise up in defense. All the careful analysis of his father's limitations, his mother's conditional love, his sister's dismissiveness. All the compassionate understanding of why they were the way they were. All the hope that transformation would somehow make them different. The story you keep telling isn't protecting you—it's keeping you small.

"The truth is that they won't change," he said finally. "No matter how much I grow. No matter what I learn. No matter how beautiful my heart becomes or how strong I get. My father will still think I'm a disappointment. My mother will still love me conditionally. Elara will still dismiss what I care about. Going home won't be different because I've transformed. It will be exactly the same."

Erenne was quiet for a moment before speaking. "And you've known this for a while."

"Yes. Since—" He stopped, feeling memory surface. "Since the storm. When I rescued Tavis and he burned his seventeen pages of prepared speeches. I knew then that all my preparation for going home was the same thing. Elaborate justification for why this time would be different. But it won't be. And I've been lying to myself about that ever since."

They ate together and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting in how he held his journey. Stop lying to yourself. He'd been telling himself a story about transformation leading to homecoming. About growing strong enough that his family would finally see him. About the journey changing him so completely that returning would be triumphant validation of everything he'd become. But that story wasn't true. Was never true. Was just elaborate avoidance of the actual truth he'd been pretending not to see.

The Do list felt pointed. Candles, Notebooks, Clean slates. Light up what you've been keeping in darkness—stop hiding from what you know. Notebooks—write down the truth you've been avoiding so you can't pretend you don't see it. Clean slates—let go of the old story so you can see what's actually there. All of it pointing toward the same thing. Today, name the thing you've been pretending not to see.

"I think I've been afraid that if I admit the truth, the journey becomes pointless," he said quietly. "Like if going home won't be different, then what was all this growth for? What was the point of transforming if it doesn't change anything back in Millhaven?"

"But the growth wasn't for them. Was it?"

Sefiathan felt something crack open in his chest. She was right. He'd been telling himself the journey was about becoming strong enough to go home. But that was the lie. The journey had never been about his family. Had been about him. About learning to take up space and trust his instincts and let his beautiful heart be visible. About recognizing his worth wasn't determined by his father's judgment or his mother's conditional love or his sister's dismissal. The transformation was real. The growth was real. But it wasn't going to change his family. Only him.

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this truth settling through him like weight he'd been avoiding. You know what's true. You've known for a while. He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and immediately his mind started generating justifications again. Maybe his family would be different. Maybe the transformation would change something. Maybe he was being too harsh, too absolute, too pessimistic about homecoming.

But he caught himself. Stop lying to yourself. Those weren't wisdom or hope or compassion. Were avoidance. Elaborate justifications to not name the thing he'd been pretending not to see. The truth was simple and had been true all along. His family wouldn't change. Going home wouldn't be triumphant validation. The journey was transforming him, but it wouldn't transform them. And he'd known that since the storm. Maybe even before the storm. Had been lying to himself about it because admitting the truth felt like admitting the journey was pointless.

The Don't list felt like warning against familiar traps. Sugarcoating, Rose-colored glasses, Half-measures. Don't soften the truth to make it more palatable. Don't view the situation through optimistic distortion. Don't try to split the difference between the lie and the truth. The reading was demanding he name what was actually there without elaborate justification or protective softening.

Afternoon brought Korrin to the creek and when he sat down beside Sefiathan there was unusual gravity in his expression.

"You look like you've been wrestling with something heavy."

"The reading said I need to stop lying to myself. That the story I keep telling isn't protecting me, it's keeping me small. That I need to name the thing I've been pretending not to see." Sefiathan took a breath. "The thing is—I've been telling myself that going home will be different. That my transformation will change how my family sees me. But that's not true. They won't change. And I've known that for months."

Korrin was quiet for a long moment. "What made you start lying to yourself about it?"

"Because if I admit they won't change, then—" Sefiathan felt his voice catch. "Then what was the point? What was all this growth for if it doesn't change anything back home? What was the journey for if going back will be exactly the same?"

"The journey was for you. Not for them."

"I know that. Logically I know that. But I've been telling myself this story where I transform enough and then they finally see me. Where the growth makes them different. Where going home becomes validation instead of—" He stopped, feeling the truth pressing up. "Instead of just going back to people who will never think I'm enough."

"Maybe that's the thing you've been pretending not to see. Not just that they won't change. But that going home might not be what you actually want."

Sefiathan felt something fundamental shift in his understanding. Korrin was right. He'd been lying to himself about more than just whether his family would change. Had been lying about whether returning to Millhaven was actually his goal. The whole year he'd been telling himself this was a journey toward homecoming. Toward going back transformed and finally being seen. But maybe that was never what he actually wanted. Maybe he'd just been telling himself that story because he didn't know what else the journey could be for.

They sat by the creek together and Sefiathan felt the elaborate justifications falling away. Stop lying to yourself. His family wouldn't change. The journey wouldn't change them. Going home wouldn't be triumphant validation. And maybe—maybe he didn't actually want to go home at all. Maybe that had been the lie all along. Not just that transformation would change his reception but that Millhaven was where he belonged. That his family were the people he needed to prove himself to. That going back was the point.

Evening brought them to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with less elaborate justification than usual.

"I've been lying to myself about going home. Telling myself the journey would transform me enough that my family would finally see me clearly. But they won't change. And I think—I think maybe I don't actually want to go back at all. Maybe that's been the real lie. That Millhaven is where I belong."

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling October moving forward. Fifteen days into this month and learning about walls blocking sunlight and now about lies keeping him small. About recognizing that the elaborate justifications he'd been constructing weren't wisdom but avoidance. About naming the truth he'd been pretending not to see—that his family wouldn't change, that the journey wouldn't transform them, that maybe going home wasn't actually what he wanted. Stop lying to yourself. Tomorrow would bring new lessons. But tonight he would rest in this uncomfortable truth. That the story he'd been telling himself about triumphant homecoming was a lie. That his family would stay exactly as they were. That the journey was transforming him but wouldn't transform them. And that maybe—maybe the point wasn't going back at all. Maybe it was learning he didn't have to.
