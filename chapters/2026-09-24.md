# **Day 266 — September 24, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twenty-fourth morning of September and the reading landed like mirror held too close to his face: Observe your feelings about clinginess. That push-pull feeling in your chest isn't weakness—it's information. Notice how you label others "clingy" when they need more, yet secretly crave that same closeness. Your careful distance keeps everyone at arm's length, including yourself.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle uncomfortably accurate. Five days ago the reading had told him he was on his own journey, that others wanted him to follow their maps but didn't understand his terrain, that doubt wasn't weakness but the sound of old ties breaking. And he'd been practicing that—trusting his own navigation, recognizing that his path belonged to him alone, being brave enough to walk unfamiliar terrain without needing everyone to understand it.

But underneath that independence had been this other thing. This careful distance he maintained. This arm's length he kept people at while simultaneously craving closeness he wouldn't let himself have. Notice how you label others "clingy" when they need more, yet secretly crave that same closeness. The reading wasn't being gentle. Was pointing directly at hypocrisy he'd been maintaining without recognizing it. The push-pull feeling in his chest. Wanting connection while judging people who wanted it from him. Craving closeness while keeping everyone—including himself—at careful distance.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling resistance rise. Because he didn't think of himself as someone who labeled people clingy. Didn't think of himself as someone who kept distance. But as he sat with the reading's accusation, memories surfaced that suggested otherwise. Mira asking for more time together and him feeling suffocated. His mother wanting him to visit more often and him thinking she was needy. Korrin offering deeper friendship and him pulling back slightly, keeping things casual, maintaining space.

He'd thought he was just protecting his independence. Just maintaining healthy boundaries. Just not letting people demand too much of his energy. But the reading reframed it. Your careful distance keeps everyone at arm's length, including yourself. He wasn't protecting independence. Was avoiding closeness. Was judging in others the very need he secretly carried himself. Was maintaining distance not because it was healthy but because wanting closeness felt dangerous. Felt like weakness. Felt like being the clingy one.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she glanced at his expression and raised an eyebrow.

"Complicated reading this morning?"

"It said to observe my feelings about clinginess. Said I label others clingy when they need more while secretly craving that same closeness. Said my careful distance keeps everyone at arm's length, including myself."

Erenne set down the kettle and looked at him carefully. "And you don't want that to be true."

"I don't think of myself as someone who keeps distance. I thought I was just maintaining boundaries. Not letting people be too demanding."

"But maybe you've been using boundaries as excuse to avoid closeness you actually want."

The words landed uncomfortably accurate. Maybe he had. Maybe every time someone wanted more from him—more time, more attention, more emotional availability—he'd labeled that as clingy instead of recognizing it as normal human need for connection. Maybe his careful distance wasn't healthy independence. Was fear dressed up as boundaries. Was judgment of needs he shared but wouldn't admit to having.

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. Pick your battles, Layering, Aromatherapy. Permission to choose what mattered instead of maintaining distance from everything. To build complexity instead of keeping things simple and distant. To engage his senses, to be present in his body instead of carefully removed from actual experience. Pick your battles—what if he could let some closeness in without it meaning total surrender of independence? Layering—what if relationships could have depth and complexity instead of being kept at safe surface level? Aromatherapy—what if he could be present to actual sensory experience of connection instead of maintaining careful abstract distance?

"I think I've been afraid of being the clingy one," he said quietly. "So I've kept everyone at distance and judged people who wanted closeness as needy or demanding."

"But you want closeness too."

"Yes. I do. I just—I didn't want to admit that because it felt like weakness."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this observation settling through him like exposure. The push-pull feeling in your chest isn't weakness—it's information. What was that feeling informing him of? He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and immediately started cataloging moments when he'd felt that push-pull. Wanting connection. Pulling away when it was offered. Craving closeness. Judging people who needed it. The contradiction lived in his chest like constant tension he'd been trying to resolve by choosing distance. By keeping everyone at arm's length. By pretending he didn't need what he judged others for wanting.

But the reading said that push-pull was information. Not weakness. Not something to resolve by choosing one side. Information about what he actually needed versus what he was allowing himself to have. He wanted closeness. Wanted depth. Wanted people to know him and want to spend time with him and ask for his attention. But he'd been treating those wants as shameful. As clingy. As something to hide and judge in others when he saw it reflected back.

The Don't list felt pointed. Naivety, Hibernation, Lost causes. Don't pretend this pattern isn't real. Don't withdraw from the work of examining it. Don't dismiss people who want closeness from you as lost causes while secretly wishing you could be that honest about your own needs. The reading was giving permission to observe his feelings while warning against avoiding what that observation revealed. Against retreating into isolation to escape the discomfort. Against labeling connection as impossible instead of examining why he kept it at arm's length.

Afternoon brought Korrin to the creek and they sat together in comfortable silence that felt different now that Sefiathan was observing his own patterns. Because this was closeness. This quiet companionship. This willingness to just be together without agenda. And Sefiathan noticed himself feeling that push-pull. Wanting this connection. Feeling urge to create distance before it became too much. Before Korrin expected things from him. Before he revealed how much he actually craved this kind of presence.

"Can I ask you something?" Sefiathan said finally.

"Always."

"Do you think I keep people at arm's length?"

Korrin was quiet for long moment. "Yes. You do. Not in cruel way. But you maintain careful distance. Like you're afraid of needing too much or being needed too much."

"The reading said I label others clingy when they need more while secretly craving that same closeness."

"And is that true?"

Sefiathan felt something tight in his chest. "Yes. It's true. I judge people for wanting connection while wanting it desperately myself. I keep everyone at distance because I'm afraid of being the clingy one. Of being too much. Of needing more than people want to give."

"But by keeping distance you guarantee you don't get what you need."

The observation landed simple and devastating. By keeping everyone at arm's length he'd guaranteed he wouldn't be too clingy. Wouldn't be too needy. Wouldn't want too much. But he'd also guaranteed he wouldn't get the closeness he actually craved. The connection he judged in others while wanting it himself. Your careful distance keeps everyone at arm's length, including yourself. He'd been so focused on not being clingy that he'd isolated himself from the very thing he wanted most.

"I think I'm afraid," Sefiathan said quietly. "Afraid that if I let people close they'll see how much I need and it will be too much. They'll think I'm clingy or demanding or exhausting."

"Or maybe they'll just see someone who wants connection. Which is normal. Which everyone wants."

They sat by the creek and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting. That push-pull feeling in your chest isn't weakness—it's information. The information was this: he wanted closeness. Wanted depth. Wanted to be known and to know others. And he'd been treating that want as shameful. As something to hide. As evidence he was too needy, too clingy, too much. But everyone wanted connection. Everyone needed closeness. The difference was some people admitted it and asked for it and let themselves be vulnerable to wanting it. And he'd been labeling those people as clingy while secretly envying their honesty. Their willingness to need. Their courage to want closeness without hiding it behind careful distance.

Evening brought him back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with less defense than usual.

"I've been observing my feelings about clinginess. Recognizing that I judge people for wanting closeness while craving it desperately myself. That my careful distance keeps everyone at arm's length, including myself."

"That's brave work."

"It doesn't feel brave. Feels like seeing something I've been avoiding seeing."

"That's what bravery is. Looking at what you've been keeping at arm's length."

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling September moving toward its end. Twenty-four days into this month and the lessons kept exposing patterns he'd maintained without recognizing them. He'd been so focused on not being too much that he'd made himself too distant. Had been so afraid of being clingy that he'd kept everyone at arm's length. Had judged in others the very needs he carried himself. The push-pull in his chest wasn't weakness. Was information about what he wanted versus what he allowed himself to have. Tomorrow he would keep observing. Keep noticing when he labeled others as needing too much while wanting that same closeness himself. Keep examining his careful distance. Keep being brave enough to see what he'd been keeping at arm's length—including his own legitimate need for connection, for closeness, for depth that didn't make him clingy or too much. Just made him human. Just made him someone who wanted what everyone wanted. And there was no shame in that. Only in the distance he'd maintained to avoid admitting it.
