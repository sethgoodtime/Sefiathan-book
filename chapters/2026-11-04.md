# **Day 307 — November 4, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his fourth morning of November and the reading landed like permission to stop pretending: Sexuality, as you know it, does not exist. The labels you've used to organize your desires are too small for what you actually want. Drop the categories. That tension in your chest isn't confusion—it's recognition of what you've always known but wouldn't admit.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle with strange resonance even though the literal content didn't quite fit. He wasn't wrestling with sexuality in the obvious sense. But the core of what the reading was saying—the labels you've used to organize your desires are too small for what you actually want—that landed with uncomfortable precision. Because he had been using labels that were too small. Not about sexuality but about everything. About what he wanted from life, from connection, from himself. He'd been trying to fit his desires into categories that made sense to other people instead of admitting what he actually wanted.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling this prompt working through him. Yesterday he'd recognized he was performing for ghosts, that the same moves that once made him magnificent now made him look desperate because he was still trying to win approval from people who weren't watching. And today the reading was pointing at something related but different. About the labels he'd been using to organize his desires. About categories that were too small. About that tension in his chest being recognition of what he'd always known but wouldn't admit.

What had he always known but wouldn't admit? That he didn't want what his family wanted for him. That belonging in Millhaven had never been the goal even when he told himself it was. That the journey wasn't about transformation or purpose or becoming someone impressive. That what he actually wanted was—what? He didn't even know. Because he'd spent so long trying to fit his desires into acceptable categories instead of letting them be what they were.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she was already watching him with knowing eyes.

"What's today's reading?"

"That the labels I've used to organize my desires are too small for what I actually want. That I need to drop the categories. That the tension in my chest isn't confusion—it's recognition of what I've always known but wouldn't admit."

Erenne poured tea slowly. "What have you always known but wouldn't admit?"

Sefiathan felt his throat tighten. "I don't know. That's the problem. I've been so busy trying to make my desires fit into categories that make sense—wanting family approval, wanting to belong somewhere, wanting purpose, wanting the journey to mean something—that I don't actually know what I want underneath all those acceptable labels."

"Drop the categories. That tension in your chest isn't confusion—it's recognition."

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the reading pressing against something he'd been avoiding. The labels you've used to organize your desires are too small. What labels had he been using? Wanting to be seen. Wanting to belong. Wanting to matter. Wanting the journey to transform him. All reasonable desires. All things people understood. But were they what he actually wanted? Or just the closest acceptable categories to something deeper and stranger that he wouldn't let himself admit?

The Do list felt deliberately expansive. Ferris wheel, Good morning texts, Blindfolds. Ferris wheel—go in circles but see from new heights. Good morning texts—simple connection without performance. Blindfolds—stop trying to see everything clearly, trust what you feel. All of it pointing toward dropping categories. Toward letting desires be strange and uncategorized and real instead of fitting them into labels that made sense.

"I think I've been trying to want the right things," he said quietly. "Trying to organize my desires into categories that would make my family proud or make me seem like I had my life together or make the journey look meaningful. But what if what I actually want doesn't fit into any of those categories? What if it's something stranger?"

"Then it's something stranger. And you can admit that instead of pretending your desires are smaller than they are."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this prompt unsettling everything he thought he understood about what he wanted. He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and let himself feel that tension in his chest. The one the reading said wasn't confusion but recognition. What was he recognizing? What had he always known but wouldn't admit?

That he didn't want a normal life. That he never had. That the journey wasn't about finding where he belonged because belonging had never been what he actually wanted. That connection mattered to him but not the way other people talked about it—not about having lots of friends or being part of a community but about those rare moments of genuine seeing and being seen. That he didn't want purpose in the way people usually meant it—some grand destiny or important work—but something harder to name. Something about existing strangely and paying attention intensely and letting that be enough.

The labels you've used to organize your desires are too small. Yes. All the labels were too small. He'd been trying to fit vast, formless wanting into categories like "family acceptance" and "belonging" and "purpose" when what he actually wanted was—what? To be allowed to exist oddly. To have his strange intensity be met instead of managed. To stop performing magnificence for ghosts and just be genuinely seen by the few people who could actually handle that. To let the journey be what it was instead of needing it to justify itself.

The Don't list felt pointed. Wait by the phone, Thumb twiddling, Divided attention. Don't wait for permission from ghosts. Don't distract yourself from what you're recognizing. Don't split your attention between what you actually want and what you think you should want. The reading was asking him to stay present with this uncomfortable recognition instead of retreating into acceptable categories.

He sat by the creek and let himself feel what happened when he dropped the categories. When he stopped trying to organize his desires into labels that made sense. The tension in his chest shifted, became something less like confusion and more like—yes. Recognition. This was what he'd always known. That his desires were strange and intense and didn't fit into normal categories. That what he wanted from life wasn't what most people wanted and that was fine, actually. That the journey didn't need to give him belonging or purpose or transformation because those weren't what he'd been looking for underneath the acceptable labels.

Afternoon brought Korrin to the creek and when he sat down Sefiathan was unusually still, feeling this recognition settling through him.

"The reading said the labels I've used to organize my desires are too small for what I actually want. That I need to drop the categories. That the tension in my chest isn't confusion—it's recognition of what I've always known but wouldn't admit."

Korrin watched him carefully. "What have you always known?"

"That I don't want what most people want. That I never did. I've been trying to fit my desires into acceptable categories—family approval, belonging, purpose—but those labels are too small. What I actually want is stranger than that. Harder to name. Something about being allowed to exist oddly and have my intensity be met instead of managed and stop performing for ghosts and just—be genuinely seen by people who can actually handle that."

"That tension in your chest isn't confusion—it's recognition."

"Yes. I've always known this. Known I was different in ways that didn't fit normal categories. But I kept trying to make my desires smaller, more acceptable, easier to explain. And now I'm recognizing that was the problem. My desires aren't too small. The categories are."

They sat together and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting in how he understood his own wanting. He'd spent his whole life trying to organize his desires into labels other people would understand. Trying to make his strangeness fit into acceptable categories. But the labels were too small. What he actually wanted was vast and formless and didn't have neat names. Drop the categories. Just let himself want what he wanted without needing it to make sense to anyone else.

Evening brought them back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with honesty that felt less rehearsed than usual.

"I've been trying to organize my desires into categories that were too small. Family approval, belonging, purpose—all labels that made sense but weren't what I actually wanted. What I actually want is stranger. About existing oddly and being genuinely seen and letting my intensity be met instead of managed. About the journey not needing to justify itself because transformation wasn't the goal underneath the acceptable labels."

"And what happens when you drop the categories?"

"I recognize what I've always known. That I'm different in ways that don't fit normal wanting. That my desires are vast and strange and that's fine. That I don't need them to make sense to ghosts who aren't watching."

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling November teaching him about categories and labels and recognition. About understanding the labels he'd used to organize his desires were too small for what he actually wanted. About that tension in his chest being recognition, not confusion—the recognition that he'd always known his wanting was strange and formless and didn't fit acceptable categories but had been trying to make it smaller anyway. About permission to drop the categories. To let his desires be vast and odd and hard to name. To stop organizing them into labels that would make sense to ghosts.

Tomorrow would bring new lessons. But tonight he would rest in this recognition. His desires were too big for categories like belonging and purpose and family approval. What he actually wanted was stranger—about existing intensely and being genuinely seen and letting that be enough. The tension in his chest wasn't confusion about what he wanted. It was recognition that he'd always known but wouldn't admit. That his wanting had never fit normal categories and never would. That the labels were too small and always had been. And that dropping them—letting his desires be vast and formless and strange—was what came next. Not performing for ghosts. Not fitting into acceptable categories. Just being odd and intense and genuinely seen by the few people who mattered. The people who would follow him into whatever came next because they could handle his strangeness. Because they saw it and stayed.
