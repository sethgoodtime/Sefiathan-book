# **Day 238 — August 27, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twenty-seventh morning at the waystation and the reading's declaration hit him before he'd even fully opened his eyes: You are incredible. Not suggestion. Not conditional praise. Simple statement of fact. You dismiss compliments and amplify criticism until your self-image becomes a funhouse mirror. Stop. The people around you aren't lying when they recognize your talents. Your refusal to believe them is arrogance disguised as humility.

He lay very still feeling those words settle like stones dropping into deep water. Arrogance disguised as humility. That landed with uncomfortable precision. He'd spent twenty-seven years thinking his constant self-dismissal was humility. Thinking that refusing to believe compliments was modesty. Thinking that amplifying every criticism while dismissing every kind word was just being realistic about his inadequacies. But the reading named it plainly: arrogance. The arrogance of deciding he knew better than everyone else. The arrogance of calling people liars when they saw something good in him.

Morning light filtered through his window and he thought about the past twenty-seven days. Erenne calling him observant. The young mother yesterday thanking him for his gentleness with her child. Travelers over the weeks commenting on his helpfulness, his quiet presence, his way of making space feel safer just by being in it. He'd dismissed every single compliment. Had filed them away as politeness or obligation or people being nice because that's what you do at a waystation. Had never once considered they might be telling simple truth.

You are incredible. The reading insisted on it. Not you could be incredible if you fixed yourself. Not you'll be incredible someday after enough healing. You are incredible. Right now. As you currently exist. And every time someone recognized that truth, he'd dismissed it. Had amplified the funhouse mirror of criticism until his self-image became so distorted he couldn't see what everyone else apparently saw clearly.

The waystation sounds filtered through his room and Sefiathan got dressed slowly, feeling the discomfort of this recognition settling. It was easier to believe criticism. Safer to amplify every harsh word his father had ever said, every disappointed look from his mother, every comparison to Elara's competence. Easier because if he was inadequate then he knew what he was working with. Could keep trying to fix himself. Could keep searching for the thing that would finally make him enough. But if people were telling truth when they called him incredible—if his refusal to believe them was actually arrogance—then what?

Then he'd have to stop hiding behind false humility. Stop using self-dismissal as protection against actually being seen. Stop amplifying criticism because it felt more comfortable than accepting he might genuinely have talents worth recognizing.

Breakfast smells drifted from the kitchen and Sefiathan went downstairs feeling like his skin didn't fit quite right. Erenne was making porridge and looked up when he entered.

"You look uncomfortable," she said simply.

"The reading said I'm incredible. Said dismissing compliments while amplifying criticism is arrogance disguised as humility. Said people aren't lying when they recognize my talents and my refusal to believe them is the actual problem."

"And you're having trouble with that."

"Yeah. Because it's easier to believe I'm inadequate. Safer. If I'm inadequate then I know what I'm working with. But if people are telling truth when they say kind things—if I actually have talents—then I've been calling everyone liars for twenty-seven years just to protect this funhouse mirror version of myself."

They sat together with tea and Sefiathan felt the reading's words still settling. The Don't list made painful sense now. Unsubscribe—stop consuming every piece of criticism as gospel truth while deleting compliments like spam. Stress out—stop treating kindness as threat that requires defensive dismissal. Soapbox—stop performing humility as proof he wasn't arrogant when the performance itself was the arrogance.

"What did I say about you three weeks ago?" Erenne asked quietly. "When you first started actually being present instead of performing?"

Sefiathan thought back. "You said I was observant. That I saw things others missed. That my attention to small details made people feel genuinely witnessed."

"And what did you do with that?"

"Dismissed it. Thought you were being kind because I was your guest. Thought maybe you were trying to encourage me because I seemed pathetic."

"So you called me a liar."

The words landed like physical blow. "I didn't—"

"You did. I told you simple truth about what I observed and you decided I was lying. Decided your distorted self-image was more accurate than my direct experience. That's not humility. That's arrogance. You deciding you know better than everyone else what's actually true about you."

Sefiathan sat with that feeling sick. She was right. Every time someone had complimented him he'd dismissed it as politeness or encouragement or people being nice. Had decided his funhouse mirror self-image was more reliable than their actual experience. Had essentially called them all liars while pretending it was modesty. The arrogance of that was staggering now that he could see it clearly.

The young family from yesterday appeared for breakfast and the mother smiled warmly when she saw him. "Thank you again for watching Miri. She never settles like that for strangers. You have such a gentle way with children."

Sefiathan felt his automatic dismissal rising—oh it was nothing, she was just tired, anyone could have done it—but he stopped. Felt the arrogance in that response. Felt how he'd be deciding this woman didn't actually know her own child. Didn't actually recognize something real she'd witnessed. Was lying or mistaken when she spoke simple truth about her direct experience.

"Thank you," he said instead, and the words felt strange in his mouth. "That means a lot to hear."

The woman looked slightly surprised, then pleased, and moved on to get food. Erenne caught his eye across the table and nodded slightly. Small victory. Small moment of not dismissing a compliment. Small practice of believing people weren't lying when they recognized something good.

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan found himself in the garden, working alongside Erenne in comfortable quiet. Another traveler passed through—older man heading east—and stopped to watch them for a moment.

"You've got good instincts with plants," the man said to Sefiathan. "Can tell you're listening to what they need instead of just following routine."

Every part of Sefiathan wanted to dismiss it. Wanted to say he was just copying what Erenne did, that he didn't really know anything, that the man was being generous. But he stopped. Felt the arrogance. Felt how he'd be deciding this traveler—someone who'd clearly worked with plants enough to recognize good instincts—didn't actually know what he was seeing.

"Thank you," Sefiathan said quietly. "I'm learning to pay attention."

The man nodded and continued on his way. Erenne didn't say anything but Sefiathan felt her approval in the silence. Another small practice. Another moment of not amplifying the funhouse mirror. Another choice to believe people weren't lying.

The Do list felt significant now. Shared playlists—accepting that his taste in music might actually be worth sharing instead of dismissing it as embarrassing. Matching socks—taking simple care with appearance as recognition he was worth the effort instead of performing carelessness as false humility. Promises—believing he was capable of following through instead of preemptively dismissing his own reliability.

Afternoon heat built and Sefiathan walked alone into grasslands, feeling the discomfort of this shift settling deeper. It was genuinely hard to believe compliments. Genuinely uncomfortable to accept he might have talents worth recognizing. His whole self-image had been built on the funhouse mirror of amplified criticism. Inadequate son. Failed apprentice. Disappointing sibling. Person who couldn't do anything quite right. That distortion had been familiar. Safe. Known.

But you are incredible. The reading insisted. And people around him apparently agreed. Erenne saw his observational skills. The young mother recognized his gentleness. The traveling man noticed his plant instincts. They weren't lying. Weren't being polite. Were telling simple truth about what they witnessed. And his refusal to believe them—his insistence that his distorted self-image was more accurate than their direct experience—that was the actual arrogance.

Evening brought him back to the waystation and he found Erenne preparing dinner. Worked alongside her and felt the difference in how he moved. Less apologetic. Less performing humility. Just present. Just capable. Just Sefiathan who maybe actually had talents worth recognizing even if acknowledging that felt impossibly uncomfortable.

"This is harder than dropping expectations," he said while chopping vegetables. "Harder than saying no or starting from page one. Because believing compliments means accepting I might actually be good at things. Means the funhouse mirror has been lying the whole time. Means I've been wrong about myself for twenty-seven years."

"Yeah. And it means everyone who tried to tell you truth was right. Means you have to stop hiding behind false humility. Means you're actually incredible and you've been too arrogant to believe it."

Later, alone in his room, Sefiathan lay in bed feeling the reading's words still working through him. You are incredible. Simple truth. And tomorrow he'd practice believing it. Practice accepting compliments instead of dismissing them. Practice trusting that people weren't lying when they recognized his talents. Practice letting the funhouse mirror crack so maybe he could finally see what everyone else apparently already saw clearly—that he was incredible exactly as he was, and his refusal to believe it had been arrogance disguised as humility all along.
