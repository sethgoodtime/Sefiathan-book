# **Day 239 — August 28, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twenty-eighth morning at the waystation and the reading's directive hit him with uncomfortable clarity: Just give them a call. Don't overthink it. That person you've been avoiding? They miss hearing from you. Your reasons for not calling sound bigger in your head than they actually are. Pick up the phone.

He lay in bed feeling that land and immediately knew who the reading meant. Not his family—he'd been writing letters, slow careful bridge-building that felt manageable. Not Korrin or Tavis—they'd parted ways weeks ago with genuine warmth and promises to cross paths again. The person he'd been avoiding was Thessaly. The fae woman who'd called him "oddball" months ago at the Singing Stones. Who'd said his strangeness was unforgettable. Who'd given him her name with clear invitation to reach out. Who he'd thought about dozens of times over the months and never once contacted because the reasons for not calling sounded massive in his head.

What would he even say? She was fae and incredible and had probably forgotten him entirely. Or worse—remembered him but been disappointed he never followed up. Or had just been being polite when she said to stay in touch. Or would think he was presumptuous for actually reaching out. Or would feel obligated to be kind when really she had more important things to do than talk to some human she'd met once. The reasons stacked up familiar and convincing and he realized the reading was naming them exactly: they sounded bigger in his head than they actually were.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan got dressed slowly, feeling yesterday's lesson about arrogance and humility connecting to this. Deciding Thessaly had been lying when she said she'd like to hear from him again—that was the same arrogance. Deciding his reasons for not calling were more valid than her expressed desire for contact—that was deciding he knew better than she did what she actually wanted. Your reasons for not calling sound bigger in your head than they actually are. Simple truth. He'd been overthinking it for months. Building elaborate narratives about why contact was impossible or inappropriate or unwanted when the actual truth was much simpler: she'd said to reach out, and he hadn't.

Breakfast smells drifted from the kitchen and Sefiathan went downstairs feeling nervous in a way he hadn't felt in weeks. Erenne was making eggs and looked up when he entered.

"You look anxious," she said simply.

"The reading said to call someone I've been avoiding. Said my reasons for not calling sound bigger in my head than they actually are. And it's right. There's someone I've been meaning to contact for months and I keep not doing it because I've built this whole story about why it's complicated when actually it's not."

"So call them."

"It's not that simple—" He stopped. Heard himself. Felt the overthinking already starting. "Actually it is that simple. I'm making it complicated. She said to reach out. I haven't. That's the whole story."

They sat together with tea and Sefiathan felt the reading's words settling more completely. The Don't list made painful sense. Quill pens—stop making simple communication into elaborate formal production that required perfect execution. Sheet music—stop following rigid methodology for how connection was supposed to work. Methodologies—stop applying complex frameworks to something as basic as picking up the phone and talking to someone who said they wanted to hear from you.

"What are you actually afraid of?" Erenne asked quietly.

Sefiathan sat with that question feeling the real fear underneath all the elaborate reasons. "That she won't remember me the way I remember her. That our interaction mattered more to me than it did to her. That reaching out will reveal I built something bigger in my head than what actually existed between us."

"And if that's true?"

"Then I'll know. And it'll hurt. But at least I'll stop carrying this weird avoidance that's been taking up space for months."

Midmorning brought warm sun and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands alone, needing movement to think through this. Thessaly had been one of those people who'd shifted something fundamental. Who'd seen his strangeness and called it unforgettable instead of embarrassing. Who'd validated that his oddness wasn't something to apologize for. Who'd made him feel genuinely seen in a way that had stuck with him through hundreds of miles and months of travel. And he'd never contacted her because the reasons for not calling had sounded so convincing in his head.

She's fae and I'm human—different worlds. She was just being polite. She probably meets dozens of interesting people. I'm not special. She said that to everyone. I'd be bothering her. She's busy with important fae matters. What would we even talk about? It's been too long now. She'll think I'm weird for reaching out after months of silence. The reasons spiraled familiar and each one sounded reasonable taken individually but together they formed a wall of overthinking that kept him from doing the simple thing: just reaching out.

That person you've been avoiding? They miss hearing from you. The reading insisted on this and Sefiathan felt how arrogant it was to decide that wasn't true. To decide Thessaly had been lying when she gave him her name. To decide his elaborate reasons for not calling were more accurate than her actual words. Pick up the phone. Don't overthink it.

But he didn't have a phone. Didn't have any normal way to contact someone who lived in fae territory in the Emerald Expanse. He'd need to send a message through proper channels—find someone traveling that direction, write a letter, hope it reached her. The logistics immediately felt complicated enough to justify more delay but he stopped himself. Felt the overthinking starting again. Felt how he was using practical obstacles as excuse for emotional avoidance.

The Do list felt pointed now. Talking points—permission to have actual conversation instead of scripting everything perfectly. Interludes—allowing space for natural pauses and organic flow instead of forcing constant productivity. Rereading—going back to revisit what had actually been said instead of relying on anxious memory that distorted everything.

He walked back to the waystation and found Erenne in the garden. Sat down beside her and said simply, "I need to send a message to someone in the Emerald Expanse. Do you know anyone heading that direction?"

Erenne looked at him with slight smile. "Merchant caravan coming through tomorrow, heading northeast. They pass near the Expanse. You could send a letter."

"That simple?"

"That simple. You've been overthinking it."

That afternoon Sefiathan sat in his room with paper and felt the familiar anxiety rising. What did he even write? How did he explain months of silence? How did he make this sound casual instead of intense? How did he avoid seeming desperate or presumptuous or weird? The questions spiraled and he recognized the pattern—the same overthinking that had kept him from reaching out at all. Just give them a call. Don't overthink it.

He picked up the pen and wrote simply:

*Thessaly—*

*This is Sefiathan. We met at the Singing Stones months ago. You called me "oddball" and said my strangeness was unforgettable. You gave me your name and I've been meaning to reach out ever since but kept not doing it for reasons that sounded bigger in my head than they actually were.*

*I'm at a waystation in the Heartlands now. Learning things about myself. Getting better at believing people when they say kind things instead of dismissing compliments. Trying to stop overthinking simple connection.*

*I thought about you today and realized I've been avoiding reaching out because I was afraid our meeting mattered more to me than it did to you. But that's just another version of deciding I know better than you what's actually true. You said you'd like to hear from me. So here I am. Hearing from me.*

*I don't know what I'm hoping for exactly. Maybe just to say thank you for seeing my oddness as something worth remembering. Maybe to stop carrying the weird avoidance of contact that's been taking up space. Maybe just to practice doing the simple thing instead of overthinking it.*

*If you want to write back, I'll be here for a while still. If not, that's okay too. Either way, thank you for the way you saw me that day.*

*—Sefiathan*

He read it over once and felt the urge to revise, to make it more eloquent, to add explanations or apologies or careful hedging. But he stopped. This was the simple version. The honest version. The version that didn't overthink it. He folded the letter and addressed it to Thessaly in the Emerald Expanse, trusting the merchant caravan would know how to find a fae woman with that name.

Evening brought him downstairs where Erenne was preparing dinner. He helped in comfortable silence and felt the anxiety of the letter sitting sealed in his room. Tomorrow he'd give it to the caravan. Tomorrow it would be out of his hands. Tomorrow he'd have done the simple thing instead of spending more months overthinking.

"That wasn't so hard, was it?" Erenne asked without looking up from her cooking.

"It was terrifying. But also simple. Exactly like the reading said—my reasons for not calling sounded bigger in my head than they actually were."

"And now?"

"Now I wait. And try not to build elaborate stories about what her response will or won't mean. Try to just let it be simple."

Later, alone in his room, Sefiathan lay in bed feeling the relief of having done the thing he'd been avoiding. He'd called—or the closest equivalent. Had reached out simply instead of overthinking. Had trusted that Thessaly had meant what she said when she gave him her name. Had practiced believing people instead of deciding his anxious narratives were more accurate than their actual words. Tomorrow he'd give the letter to the merchants. And whether Thessaly wrote back or not, he'd stopped avoiding. Had stopped letting reasons that sounded big in his head keep him from simple connection. Had picked up the metaphorical phone.
