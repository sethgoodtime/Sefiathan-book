# **Day 241 — August 30, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his thirtieth morning at the waystation and the reading landed with the weight of something he'd been avoiding for months: Trust your aloneness. Stop apologizing for needing space. The right people will respect your boundaries without making you explain them. Your independence isn't a barrier to love—it's what makes your love worth having.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle through his whole body. Two days ago he'd sent the letter to Thessaly. Yesterday had been quiet—working in the garden, helping with waystation tasks, existing in the comfortable rhythm he'd built here. No response yet, which was expected. The merchant caravan had only left yesterday morning. But the reading wasn't about Thessaly's response. Was about something deeper he'd been carrying since he first left Millhaven nearly eight months ago.

He'd been apologizing for his aloneness. Treating his need for solitude like a character flaw that required explanation. Acting like his independence was something wrong with him that kept him from connecting properly with others. His father had called him withdrawn. His mother had worried about his long solitary walks. Elara had seemed confused by his contentment in his own company. And he'd internalized all of it—had started believing his aloneness was barrier instead of foundation. Had started thinking love required sacrificing the independence that made him who he was.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan got dressed slowly, feeling this truth settling. The right people will respect your boundaries without making you explain them. He thought about Korrin, who'd traveled beside him for weeks and never questioned when he needed quiet. Thought about Erenne, who'd given him space here without making him justify it. Thought about Thessaly, who'd seen his oddness and called it unforgettable, who'd never asked him to be less strange or more available or anything other than exactly what he was.

The people who mattered had never made his aloneness a problem. Had never asked him to apologize for needing space. Had never treated his independence like barrier to connection. He'd been the one doing that. Had been the one apologizing. Had been the one trying to explain or justify or soften his need for solitude as though it was something wrong with him instead of simply how he was built.

Breakfast smells drifted from the kitchen and Sefiathan went downstairs feeling this shift settling deeper. Erenne was making porridge and looked up when he entered, reading something in his face immediately.

"Different kind of morning," she said simply.

"The reading said to trust my aloneness. Said my independence isn't a barrier to love, it's what makes my love worth having. Said the right people will respect my boundaries without making me explain them."

"And you've been apologizing for needing space."

"For years. My whole life maybe. Treating my need for solitude like something wrong with me. Like if I was better at connection I wouldn't need so much time alone. But that's not true. The aloneness isn't barrier. It's foundation."

They sat together with tea and Sefiathan felt the reading's words still working through him. The Don't list made painful sense. Big reveals—stop treating his need for space like confession that required dramatic announcement. Spam—stop flooding people with explanations or justifications for boundaries that didn't need defending. Play yourself—stop performing connection in ways that betrayed his actual nature just to avoid seeming too solitary.

"What would it look like to trust your aloneness?" Erenne asked quietly. "To stop apologizing for it?"

Sefiathan sat with that question feeling something shift. "It would mean believing my independence makes my connection more valuable, not less. Means when I choose to be with someone, it's genuine choice, not obligation or fear of being alone. Means my love is worth having because it comes from someone who's complete on their own, not someone desperate to be filled up by another person."

"That's what makes your love worth having," Erenne said. "Not despite your aloneness. Because of it."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan found himself in the garden alone, hands in soil, feeling the truth of this settling completely. He'd spent his whole life thinking his solitary nature was something to overcome. Something that kept him from being the kind of person others wanted around. But that was backwards. His aloneness was what made his presence meaningful. His independence was what made his choice to connect genuine. The right people didn't need him to explain or apologize or perform connection he didn't actually feel. They respected his boundaries because they understood those boundaries made his love real.

A family arrived at the waystation—parents with three children, clearly exhausted from travel. Sefiathan watched them settle in and felt his usual pull to help, but also felt something new. Permission to choose. Permission to stay in the garden if he wanted. Permission to trust that his aloneness didn't make him selfish or withdrawn or broken. The Do list felt significant now. The blues—permission to feel melancholy without apologizing for it. Cool breeze—finding comfort in solitude without treating it as problem to fix. Windmills—steady reliable presence that didn't require constant performance.

He stayed in the garden. Chose to. Not from fear or avoidance, but from genuine desire for continued solitude. The family would be fine without his help. Erenne would handle their needs. And his choice to stay working with plants instead of immediately offering assistance wasn't failure of connection. Was honoring his actual need. Was trusting his aloneness instead of apologizing for it.

Afternoon heat built and the father from the traveling family wandered out to the garden, watching Sefiathan work for a moment before speaking. "You're good with plants. Natural instinct for what they need."

Sefiathan looked up and smiled but didn't stop working. "Thank you. I'm learning to listen to them."

"My wife wondered if you might watch our youngest for a bit. She's exhausted and the baby won't settle."

And there it was. The request. The expectation. The assumption he'd help because that's what people did. Sefiathan felt his automatic yes rising, felt the apology forming for even considering saying no, felt all his old patterns of accommodating and performing helpfulness surging up. But he stopped. Felt the difference. He didn't want to watch the baby. Wanted to stay in the garden. Wanted his solitude. And that wasn't selfish. Wasn't barrier to love. Was honoring his actual boundaries.

"I'd rather not," Sefiathan said simply. "I'm enjoying the quiet out here. But Erenne inside is wonderful with children if your wife needs help."

The father looked slightly surprised but nodded easily. "Sure, no problem. Thanks anyway." He walked back inside and Sefiathan felt the moment settle. No drama. No big reveal. No lengthy explanation required. Just simple boundary respected. The right people will respect your boundaries without making you explain them.

Evening brought him back inside where Erenne was preparing dinner. He helped in comfortable quiet and felt the difference in how he moved. Less apologetic. Less performing availability. Just present in way that honored both connection and his need for space. The family ate dinner together and the father smiled at him warmly when they crossed paths, no tension about the earlier refusal, no judgment about Sefiathan's choice to stay alone.

Later Sefiathan sat on the porch watching stars emerge and thought about Thessaly's letter traveling toward her. Thought about what he'd written—honest and simple and not apologizing for months of silence or his odd intensity or his need to reach out on his own timeline. He'd trusted his aloneness in writing it. Had let his independence show instead of softening it or explaining it away. And whatever her response, that felt right.

His independence wasn't barrier to love. Was what made his love worth having. When he chose connection, it was genuine. When he offered presence, it was real. When he said yes to helping or being with someone, it meant something because he was complete enough on his own to make real choice. The aloneness wasn't what kept him from others. Was foundation that made his connection meaningful.

Erenne joined him on the porch with tea and they sat together in comfortable silence. Eventually she spoke quietly. "You seem settled."

"I am. Been apologizing for my aloneness my whole life. Treating my need for space like character flaw. But it's not. It's what makes my presence worth having. The right people don't need me to explain that."

"No. They don't. They just see you clearly and respect what they see."

Night deepened and Sefiathan felt the month drawing toward close. August nearly finished. Eight months since leaving Millhaven. Eight months of learning he was incredible and could start from page one and didn't need to overthink simple connection. Eight months of discovering his strangeness was unforgettable and his attention was gift and his love—when he chose to offer it—was worth having precisely because it came from someone who was complete on their own.

He went to bed feeling the blues the reading had given permission for. Feeling the cool breeze of solitude. Feeling like windmill—steady presence that didn't require explanation. Tomorrow he'd keep trusting his aloneness. Keep honoring boundaries without apologizing. Keep believing his independence made his love more valuable, not less. The right people would respect that. Already had been respecting it. He just needed to trust it himself.
