# **Day 256 — September 14, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his fourteenth morning of September and the reading landed like accusation he'd been trying to avoid: Make your own life your priority. Stop volunteering for every crisis that crosses your path. Someone else can handle their own drama today. You have been neglecting your own plans and dreams while cleaning up other people's messes. Time to reverse that pattern.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle heavy in his chest. Two days ago the reading had told him it wasn't selfish to want love and support. Had insisted his craving for connection was legitimate human need, not weakness. Had said the relationships worth having could handle his vulnerability without breaking. And he'd practiced that—let Korrin carry some of his weight, dropped his independent façade, started recognizing that needing people was human rather than shameful.

But underneath that work had been this other pattern. This one he'd been maintaining so long it felt like identity rather than choice. Stop volunteering for every crisis. The words felt specific. Personal. Targeted at something he'd been doing without even recognizing he was doing it. And as he lay there, memories surfaced—all the times he'd abandoned his own needs to help someone else. All the crises he'd inserted himself into. All the drama he'd volunteered to clean up instead of focusing on his own life.

You have been neglecting your own plans and dreams while cleaning up other people's messes. That landed accurate and uncomfortable. He had been doing that. His whole life. In Millhaven he'd helped his father in the workshop instead of pursuing his own interests. Had mediated between his parents instead of building his own life. Had spent hours listening to Mira's problems while never sharing his own. And he'd told himself that was good. That was being helpful. That was proving he had value even if he wasn't competent in traditional ways.

But the reading called it neglect. Neglecting your own plans and dreams. The implication being that he had plans and dreams—or should have—and he'd been abandoning them. Prioritizing other people's crises over his own life. And the reading wasn't gentle about it. Time to reverse that pattern. Not "consider reversing." Not "maybe think about." Time to reverse it. Direct instruction. Make your own life your priority.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling resistance rise. Because making his own life priority felt selfish. Felt wrong. Felt like abandoning people who needed him. Someone else can handle their own drama today. But what if they couldn't? What if he was the only one who would help? What if walking away meant leaving people in crisis?

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she glanced at his face and raised an eyebrow.

"Complicated morning?"

"The reading said to make my own life my priority. Said I've been neglecting my plans and dreams while cleaning up other people's messes. Said to stop volunteering for every crisis."

Erenne set down the kettle. "And that feels impossible."

"It feels selfish. Like abandoning people. Someone else can handle their own drama today—but what if they can't? What if I'm the only one who will help?"

"Are you though? The only one?"

Sefiathan opened his mouth to say yes and stopped. Was he the only one? Or did he just volunteer so quickly that other people never had chance to step up? Did he insert himself into crises before anyone else could respond? Did he clean up messes before anyone else even recognized there was mess to clean?

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. Conversation piece, Work the room, Buddy system. Permission to engage with people—but on his terms. For his purposes. Conversation piece—be interesting, have something to say about his own life instead of just absorbing everyone else's stories. Work the room—move through social space deliberately instead of just standing in corner available to whoever needed him. Buddy system—find people who support his priorities instead of people whose crises consume him.

"I've been using helping as identity," he said quietly. "As proof I have value. If I'm useful to other people then it doesn't matter that I'm not competent in traditional ways."

"But that's not healthy," Erenne said. "You're not supporting people. You're neglecting yourself to prove you deserve to exist."

The words landed hard. Neglecting yourself to prove you deserve to exist. That was exactly what he'd been doing. Had been volunteering for every crisis not because he genuinely wanted to help but because helping was how he proved he had worth. Was how he justified taking up space. Was how he earned right to exist despite his inadequacies. And the reading was saying that pattern needed to reverse. That his own life needed to be priority. That other people could handle their own drama.

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this challenge settling uncomfortably. Make your own life your priority. What did that even mean? What were his plans and dreams? He'd spent so long neglecting them in favor of other people's needs that he wasn't sure he could identify them anymore. What did he actually want? What was he working toward? What life was he trying to build?

He sat by the creek and tried to access those questions. But immediately his mind started listing other people's needs instead. Korrin might want company. Erenne could probably use help preparing dinner. There were travelers at the waystation who might need conversation or support or someone to listen to their problems. His family back in Millhaven was probably still struggling with things he could help with if he just went home.

Stop. Stop volunteering for every crisis that crosses your path. Those weren't his crises. Those were other people's drama. And someone else could handle it. Could handle their own drama today. He didn't need to insert himself. Didn't need to volunteer. Didn't need to abandon his own life to prove his worth by cleaning up other people's messes.

But what was his own life? The question felt enormous. Felt impossible to answer. Because he'd been neglecting his plans and dreams for so long they felt like foreign concepts. He'd spent his whole life reactive—responding to other people's needs, inserting himself into their crises, proving his value through usefulness. He'd never learned to be proactive. To identify what he actually wanted. To make his own life priority.

The Don't list felt pointed. Voyeurism, Slingshots, Piggybacking. Don't just watch other people's lives and insert yourself when crisis happens. Don't fling yourself at problems that aren't yours to solve. Don't hitch your worth to other people's needs. The reading was giving permission to prioritize himself while warning against patterns he'd been maintaining his whole life. Against volunteering for drama that wasn't his. Against neglecting his own dreams while cleaning up everyone else's messes.

Afternoon brought moment that crystallized everything. He was sitting by the creek when he heard raised voices from the waystation—travelers arguing about something. And immediately he felt it—the pull to go help. To mediate. To insert himself into their crisis and solve it for them. To prove his value by being useful. To volunteer.

But he caught himself. Someone else can handle their own drama today. That wasn't his crisis. Those weren't his people. They could work it out themselves. Or Erenne could help if they needed external support. Or they could just argue and eventually figure it out on their own. The point was—he didn't need to volunteer. Didn't need to abandon whatever he was working on by this creek to go clean up their mess.

And what was he working on by this creek? That was harder question. Because he'd come out here without plan. Without purpose. Without dreams or goals or priorities of his own. Had just come out here to sit and think and be available in case anyone needed him. Time to reverse that pattern. If he was going to make his own life priority, he needed to actually have priorities. Needed to identify what he wanted. Needed to claim plans and dreams instead of just being endlessly available for other people's crises.

He stayed by the creek as the arguing voices faded and worked on that question. What did he actually want? What was he trying to build? He wanted to understand water's movement. Wanted to develop his own relationship with natural world instead of just learning what other people taught him. Wanted to create things—melodies, observations, understanding—that were his rather than borrowed. Wanted friendships that were mutual instead of him just absorbing everyone else's problems. Wanted to trust his own intelligence instead of constantly seeking validation.

Those were his plans. His dreams. His priorities. And he'd been neglecting them. Had been volunteering for every crisis that crossed his path instead of focusing on what he actually wanted to build. Had been cleaning up other people's messes instead of creating his own life. And the reading was right—it was time to reverse that pattern.

Evening brought him back to the waystation where Erenne was preparing dinner. And when she asked if he'd help, he noticed something different in himself. He didn't automatically say yes. Didn't immediately abandon what he was working on. Asked instead—"What do you actually need? Because I can help with specific thing but I'm also working on something and want to finish it first."

Erenne looked at him carefully and smiled. "I'm fine. You work on your thing."

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling September deepening. Fourteen days into this month and the lessons kept building—honoring the past, adapting instead of fighting, recognizing that wanting support wasn't selfish, and now making his own life priority instead of volunteering for everyone else's crises. All of it about reversing patterns he'd maintained his whole life. All of it about claiming space for his own plans and dreams instead of neglecting them to prove his worth through usefulness.

Make your own life your priority. He felt those words settling like challenge he needed to keep meeting. He'd spent decades volunteering for every crisis. Cleaning up other people's messes. Neglecting his own plans and dreams. And tomorrow he'd keep practicing different way. Keep asking what he actually wanted. Keep claiming his own priorities. Keep trusting that someone else could handle their own drama. Keep building his own life instead of just being endlessly available for everyone else's crises. The pattern needed reversing. And he was learning—slowly, carefully, with resistance and practice—how to do that. How to make his own life priority. How to stop volunteering. How to let other people handle their own drama while he focused on his own plans and dreams. Because he had plans. He had dreams. And they mattered. They deserved his attention. They deserved priority. And neglecting them to prove his worth by cleaning up messes wasn't helping anyone. Wasn't actually generous. Was just another way of abandoning himself. Time to reverse that pattern. Time to make his own life priority. Tomorrow he would keep practicing.
