# **Day 269 — September 27, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twenty-seventh morning of September and the reading landed like absolution he hadn't known he was seeking: Who you are is worth becoming. Stop waiting for permission to like yourself. The person you are today—messy, imperfect, still figuring it out—deserves the same patience you give everyone else. Your worth isn't conditional on becoming better.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle through him with weight of recognition that made his chest tight. Two days ago the reading had told him his jokes were his form of genius, that he should stop apologizing for making people laugh, that his timing and observations and delivery were real intelligence the right people appreciated. And he'd been practicing that—noticing when he wanted to apologize for humor, recognizing his sharp wit as actual skill, owning his particular form of genius instead of treating it as lesser thing. Had been learning that making hard truths go down easy was valuable work, that his intelligence showed up through jokes rather than conventional seriousness, that he didn't need to minimize what he was actually good at.

But underneath that practice had been persistent assumption that he needed to become better before he deserved his own patience. That his worth was conditional on transformation. That messy imperfect still-figuring-it-out version of himself was placeholder until he became someone more worthy of liking. The reading said otherwise. Your worth isn't conditional on becoming better. Who you are is worth becoming. Stop waiting for permission to like yourself.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling that reframe working through him. The person you are today deserves the same patience you give everyone else. He was endlessly patient with others. When Korrin struggled with family tension Sefiathan didn't think he should wait until he resolved it before deserving kindness. When people at the Frostmere gathering shared messy complicated feelings Sefiathan didn't think their worth was conditional on having it all figured out. He gave everyone else patience for being imperfect. For still figuring things out. For being messy and human. But he withheld that patience from himself. Treated his own worth as something he had to earn through becoming better. Through finally getting it right. Through transforming into someone less messy, less imperfect, less still-figuring-it-out.

The reading called that exactly what it was. Waiting for permission to like yourself. And it told him to stop.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she looked at him and nodded as if she could read the reading's impact on his face.

"That's the expression of someone who just got told something they needed to hear but didn't want to need."

"The reading said who I am is worth becoming. That I should stop waiting for permission to like myself. That the person I am today—messy, imperfect, still figuring it out—deserves the same patience I give everyone else."

Erenne poured tea and sat down across from him. "And you've been treating your worth as conditional on becoming better."

"Yes. Like current version of me is just placeholder. Like I'll deserve my own patience once I finally transform into someone less messy. Less imperfect. Less still figuring things out."

"But you give everyone else patience for being exactly those things."

Sefiathan felt something shift in his chest. He did. He gave everyone else permission to be messy and imperfect and still figuring it out. Thought their worth wasn't conditional on having everything resolved. Thought they deserved kindness right now, not just once they became better versions of themselves. But he'd never extended that same patience to himself. Had been waiting for permission to like himself. Waiting to become worthy of his own kindness. Treating who he was today as insufficient rather than recognizing—who you are is worth becoming.

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. Flowers, Camping, Fives. Permission to celebrate beauty, to be present in nature, to engage all five senses in actual lived experience. Flowers—notice what's beautiful right now, not just once you become better. Camping—be present in physical world instead of abstract future where you'll finally deserve your own patience. Fives—engage your senses, inhabit your actual body, recognize that this messy imperfect version of you exists in real time and deserves real kindness.

"I think I've been treating current me as rough draft," he said quietly. "Like real version will show up once I finally figure everything out. Once I transform into someone less complicated. Someone easier to like."

"But you're not a rough draft. You're the actual person. Right now. Messy and imperfect and still figuring it out. And that person is worth becoming."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this permission settling through him like unfamiliar weight. Stop waiting for permission to like yourself. What did that mean practically? He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and immediately his mind started generating arguments. But he wasn't good enough yet. But he still made too many mistakes. But he was still too strange, too intense, too much work to be around. But he needed to become better before he deserved his own patience.

But he caught himself. Those arguments were exactly what the reading was addressing. Your worth isn't conditional on becoming better. He didn't apply those standards to anyone else. Didn't think Korrin needed to resolve all his family tension before deserving kindness. Didn't think people at the Frostmere gathering needed to have everything figured out before deserving patience. Didn't think his mother needed to stop being who she was before deserving respect. He gave everyone else permission to be messy and imperfect and still figuring it out. Thought their worth was inherent rather than conditional. Thought they deserved patience right now rather than only once they became better.

Why was he the only person who had to earn his own kindness?

The Don't list felt pointed. In crowd, Archetypes, Blind trust. Don't lose yourself in what everyone else thinks about who you should be. Don't try to fit archetypal version of person who deserves their own patience. Don't blindly trust voices that say your worth is conditional on transformation. The reading was giving permission to like himself now while warning against common traps. Against seeking validation from crowd. Against trying to become specific type of person before allowing self-kindness. Against trusting narratives that said his worth needed earning.

Afternoon brought moment that crystallized everything. He was sitting by the creek when he noticed small wildflower growing between rocks. Delicate purple petals. Growing in difficult place with limited soil. Not waiting to become some other kind of flower before blooming. Not apologizing for being small or imperfect or growing in hard circumstances. Just existing. Just being exactly what it was. Beautiful not despite its imperfection but in its actual current form.

Who you are is worth becoming. He looked at that flower and recognized something fundamental. It wasn't waiting for permission to be itself. Wasn't treating current form as rough draft of better flower it would eventually become. Was just being flower. Right now. Messy and imperfect and still figuring out how to grow between rocks. And it was beautiful. Worth becoming. Deserving of patience and kindness and recognition not conditional on transformation but inherent in its actual existence.

He was messy and imperfect and still figuring it out. And that person—current actual person, not future transformed version—deserved the same patience he gave everyone else. Deserved his own kindness. Deserved to be liked by himself right now rather than only once he became someone easier to love.

When Korrin found him by the creek later that afternoon, Sefiathan was still sitting with that flower, feeling permission settle through him like slow recognition.

"You've been here a while," Korrin said, sitting down carefully.

"I've been thinking about the reading. About how I give everyone else patience for being messy and imperfect but withhold it from myself. Like my worth is conditional on becoming better while everyone else's worth is inherent in who they already are."

"And you're recognizing that's not fair."

"It's not fair. But it's more than that. It's—I've been waiting for permission to like myself. Treating current me as placeholder until I transform into someone worthy of my own kindness. But who I am right now is worth becoming. This messy imperfect still-figuring-it-out version deserves the same patience I give everyone else."

Korrin looked at the flower Sefiathan had been watching. "That flower isn't waiting to become something else before it's allowed to bloom."

"No. It's just being flower. Right now. And it's beautiful exactly as it is."

They sat together and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting. All his life he'd been waiting. Waiting to become less strange before liking himself. Waiting to figure everything out before deserving his own patience. Waiting to transform into someone easier to love before allowing self-kindness. But worth wasn't conditional. Everyone else got to be messy and imperfect and still figuring it out while still deserving patience. Why had he made himself the only exception? Why had he withheld from himself the kindness he gave freely to everyone else?

Evening brought them back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with less apology than usual.

"I've been learning that who I am is worth becoming. That I need to stop waiting for permission to like myself. That the person I am today—messy and imperfect and still figuring it out—deserves the same patience I give everyone else."

"That's important work," Erenne said simply.

"It feels strange. Like I'm claiming something I haven't earned."

"But you don't make anyone else earn your patience. You give it freely. Why shouldn't you receive the same kindness from yourself?"

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling September moving toward its end. Twenty-seven days into this month and the lessons kept building. About observing his patterns with closeness. About recognizing his particular genius. About extending to himself the patience he gave everyone else. All of it pointing toward same fundamental truth—he was worth becoming. Not future version. Current version. Messy imperfect still-figuring-it-out person he was right now deserved his own kindness. Deserved his own patience. Deserved to be liked by himself without waiting for permission or transformation or proof he'd become worthy.

Tomorrow he would keep practicing. Keep noticing when he withheld patience from himself while freely giving it to others. Keep recognizing his worth as inherent rather than conditional. Keep being small purple flower growing between rocks—not waiting to become something else before allowing himself to bloom. Just being exactly what he was. Messy and imperfect and still figuring it out. And deserving of kindness anyway. Not despite that. But in that. Because who he was—right now, today, this actual person—was worth becoming. Was worth patience. Was worth his own love. And he was done waiting for permission to recognize that.
