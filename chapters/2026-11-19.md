# **Day 322 — November 19, 2026**

Sefiathan woke with dawn instead of in darkness, and the difference felt significant. Yesterday's lesson about 3AM thoughts and waiting for sunrise had settled into his bones—not just intellectual understanding but lived knowledge. He'd survived the catastrophic spiral, waited for morning, watched impossible shrink to manageable. And now here was another day, another reading, another chance to practice what he was learning.

He built his fire and made tea and sat watching morning light strengthen through the trees before checking his phone. The reading landed different than yesterday's gentle instruction to wait. This one felt like confrontation. You don't know how powerful you are. Someone is trying to cut you down to size because they recognize a threat when they see one. Stop apologizing for taking up space. Your presence changes rooms and people notice.

Sefiathan stared at those words feeling something shift uncomfortably in his chest. You don't know how powerful you are. That didn't—he wasn't powerful. He was just walking. Just learning to exist without performing for ghosts. Just trying to stop shrinking himself into acceptable shapes. But the reading was insisting something else. That someone was trying to cut him down to size. That they recognized a threat. That he needed to stop apologizing for taking up space because his presence changed rooms and people notice.

He read it again, slower. Someone is trying to cut you down to size. Who? His first instinct was to look externally—his family, old friends, some enemy he hadn't recognized. But as he sat with the words he felt them pointing somewhere more uncomfortable. The someone trying to cut him down to size was himself. Had been himself this whole time. That internal voice that kept insisting he was too much, too intense, too strange. The voice that made him apologize for existing. That was the threat-recognizer. That was what noticed his power and worked constantly to diminish it.

He poured more tea and let the reading settle deeper. Your presence changes rooms and people notice. He thought about yesterday's 3AM spiral, how it had tried to convince him he was lost, failing, should turn back. That was the cutting-down-to-size impulse. That was the internal someone who recognized power and worked to shrink it. Because what if he actually was powerful? What if his presence actually did change rooms? That was terrifying. Easier to stay small. Easier to apologize. Easier to believe he was lost and failing than to recognize he might be exactly where he needed to be, doing exactly what needed doing.

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan packed up camp slowly, feeling this prompt working through him with uncomfortable precision. The Do list pointed toward messy authenticity—Off-key karaoke, Phone on silent, Messiness. Don't perform perfectly. Don't answer every demand for attention. Don't keep everything controlled and contained. The reading was asking him to be messy and present and powerful in ways that made other people—including the internal someone—uncomfortable.

He started walking, the landscape continuing its gradual shift south. Golden grasslands giving way to something more varied—scattered woods, rocky outcroppings, the air definitely warmer even as November moved toward its end. And as he walked he kept coming back to that central statement. You don't know how powerful you are. What did that mean? He'd spent his whole life convinced he had no magic, no particular gifts, nothing that made him powerful. Just this odd intensity that made people uncomfortable. This strange sideways love that didn't fit expected patterns. This way of paying attention that felt too much.

But what if—what if that was the power? What if the oddness and intensity and sideways love were exactly what made him powerful? And what if he'd spent twenty-seven years apologizing for it, cutting himself down to size, trying to diminish what other people found threatening? Including himself. Especially himself.

The Don't list felt pointed. First impressions, Pretending, Mob mentality. Don't perform for how people initially see you. Don't fake being smaller than you are. Don't shrink yourself to fit group expectations. All of it asking him to stop cutting himself down to size. To stop apologizing. To recognize that his presence changed rooms and that wasn't something to diminish.

By afternoon he'd reached a small cluster of buildings—not a village exactly but a waystation similar to where he'd spent weeks with Erenne. This one was smaller, just a main building and a few outbuildings, but as Sefiathan approached he felt the reading pressing against his usual instinct to make himself smaller. To apologize for his presence. To perform unthreatening so people wouldn't notice him too much.

An older woman was sweeping the front steps and when she looked up and saw him, something shifted in her expression. Not fear exactly but—recognition. The kind of notice the reading had mentioned. Your presence changes rooms and people notice. Sefiathan felt his instinct kick in—make yourself smaller, apologize for existing, perform harmless so she doesn't feel threatened. But instead he just stood there. Didn't shrink. Didn't apologize. Just was present.

"You look like you could use a meal," the woman said after a moment, still watching him with that careful attention. "And probably a bath. We've got both if you need them."

"Thank you," Sefiathan said, and even his voice felt different. Not performing gratitude or humility or any particular acceptable emotion. Just genuine thanks. The woman nodded and gestured him inside.

The waystation was warm and simple, smelling of bread and woodsmoke. The woman introduced herself as Marna and showed him where he could wash up, where he could eat, where he could sleep if he wanted to stay the night. And through it all Sefiathan felt the reading working. Stop apologizing for taking up space. He kept catching himself about to apologize—for the trouble, for his presence, for existing in her space—and stopping. Just accepting what was offered without diminishing himself.

Over dinner Marna asked about his journey and Sefiathan found himself answering honestly instead of performing some acceptable narrative. "I left home almost eleven months ago because I felt inadequate my whole life. I've been walking and learning and changing. I spent weeks at a waystation north of here doing slow work on rewiring old habits. Left six days ago. Walking south now without a specific destination. Just—continuing the journey."

Marna was quiet for a moment, then said, "You have a strange quality. It's not uncomfortable exactly but it's—noticeable. Makes me want to pay attention."

Your presence changes rooms and people notice. Sefiathan felt the words landing physically. This was what the reading meant. This noticing. This attention his presence created. He'd spent his whole life trying to minimize it, apologize for it, make it less threatening. Because somewhere deep down he recognized it was power and power was dangerous. Better to stay small. Better to cut himself down to size before someone else did it for him.

"I've always apologized for that quality," he said quietly. "Tried to make it smaller. Less intense. I didn't know how else to exist."

"And now?"

"Now I'm trying to stop apologizing. Trying to recognize that maybe it's not something that needs to be diminished. Maybe it's—power I didn't know I had."

Marna nodded slowly. "Someone once told me that people who threaten the status quo get cut down to size. Sometimes by others but mostly by themselves. We learn to shrink before anyone has to tell us to. Makes everyone more comfortable that way."

Evening found Sefiathan in a small room Marna had given him for the night, lying in an actual bed instead of his bedroll. He thought about that statement. Someone is trying to cut you down to size because they recognize a threat when they see one. The threat wasn't to other people exactly. It was to the status quo. To expected patterns. To comfortable assumptions about how people should exist. His strangeness, his intensity, his sideways love—those things disrupted comfort. Made people notice. Changed rooms.

And he'd learned to do the cutting himself. Had internalized the shrinking. Had apologized for taking up space before anyone had to ask him to. That was the internal someone. That was the threat-recognizer who worked constantly to diminish what made him powerful. Because power was dangerous and strangeness was threatening and being noticed meant being vulnerable to attack.

But what if he stopped? What if he stopped apologizing? What if he let his presence change rooms and let people notice? What if he recognized that you don't know how powerful you are wasn't accusation but invitation? Permission to stop cutting himself down to size. Permission to exist fully instead of apologizing for existing at all.

He lay in darkness feeling November teaching him about power and presence and the internal someone who worked to shrink what threatened comfort. About recognizing that his strangeness wasn't weakness but strength. That his intensity wasn't something to apologize for but something that changed rooms. That people noticed him not because he was doing something wrong but because his presence was genuinely powerful in ways he'd spent twenty-seven years trying to diminish.

Tomorrow he would continue south. Would practice not apologizing. Would let his presence change rooms without shrinking himself to make others comfortable. Would recognize the internal someone who tried to cut him down to size and refuse to do that work for them anymore. The reading was right. He didn't know how powerful he was. But he was starting to learn. Starting to feel what it meant to exist fully instead of apologetically. Starting to recognize that the very things he'd tried to minimize his whole life might be exactly what made him powerful. The strangeness. The intensity. The sideways love. The presence that changed rooms. The noticing that made people pay attention. All of it. Power he was just beginning to understand.
