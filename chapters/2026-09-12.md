# **Day 254 — September 12, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twelfth morning of September and the reading landed like permission he'd been waiting his entire life to receive: It isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. Behind that independent façade, you crave connection more than you admit. The relationships worth having can handle your needs without breaking. Stop treating vulnerability like weakness. Let someone carry your weight for once.

He lay in bed feeling those words work through him like breaking open something he'd kept sealed for decades. Yesterday the reading had told him to honor what happened. Had insisted his mental filing cabinet of painful memories had burst open and he needed to stop stuffing everything back in. Had said his scars told story worth hearing and running from the past kept him chained to it. And he'd spent the day letting memories surface without filing them away—his father's dismissal, Mira's betrayal, all the accumulated wounds of feeling inadequate. Had practiced honoring what happened instead of pretending it didn't matter.

But underneath that work had been this other thing. This assumption so deep he'd never questioned it: needing people was selfish. Wanting support was burden. Craving love and connection was weakness that proved he wasn't actually independent enough to survive on his own. The reading was calling that assumption out directly. It isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. Simple statement. Revolutionary permission.

Behind that independent façade, you crave connection more than you admit. Sefiathan felt that land in his chest and stay there heavy. Independent façade. He had one of those. Had built it carefully over years. Had learned to present himself as someone who didn't need anything from anyone. Who could handle everything alone. Who was fine with solitude, fine with being overlooked, fine with his friendships being surface-level and conditional. Had told himself he preferred it that way. That deep connection was too complicated, too risky, too much work.

But that was lie. Was protection. Was façade he'd built because admitting he craved connection felt dangerous. Because needing people had always resulted in disappointment. His father dismissed any need as burden. His sister had her own life and didn't have space for his feelings. Mira had abandoned their friendship the moment it required any actual support. So he'd learned to pretend he didn't need connection. Learned to present as independent. Learned to treat his craving for love and support as shameful weakness instead of legitimate human need.

The relationships worth having can handle your needs without breaking. He felt resistance rise immediately. Could they though? Every relationship he'd had suggested otherwise. Suggested that showing real need broke things. That vulnerability drove people away. That asking for support proved you were too much, too needy, too burdensome. But the reading insisted. The relationships worth having. Not all relationships. Not the ones he'd learned from. But the ones worth having—those could handle his needs. Those wouldn't break under weight of his humanity.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling this challenge settling through him. Stop treating vulnerability like weakness. Let someone carry your weight for once. That felt impossible. Not because he thought vulnerability was weakness intellectually—he'd learned enough on this journey to understand that it wasn't. But because actually letting someone carry his weight, actually admitting he needed support, actually dropping the independent façade and showing his craving for connection—that felt like inviting catastrophe. Felt like giving someone power to hurt him. Felt like proving he was exactly as inadequate as he'd always feared.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she looked at him carefully.

"That's a complicated face."

"The reading said it isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. Said behind my independent façade I crave connection more than I admit. Said the relationships worth having can handle my needs without breaking."

Erenne poured tea and sat down across from him. "And you don't believe that."

"I want to believe it. But every relationship I've had taught me the opposite. Taught me that showing need drives people away. That vulnerability breaks things. That wanting support is burden that proves I'm too much."

"So you built an independent façade."

Sefiathan nodded, feeling the weight of it. "I learned to pretend I don't need anything from anyone. To present as self-sufficient. To treat my craving for connection as shameful instead of human."

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. Utopias, Freshwater, Finish the novel. Strange combination that felt deliberate. Utopias—imagining world where connection was safe, where vulnerability was honored, where needing people wasn't treated as weakness. Freshwater—clean source, renewal, something life-giving instead of toxic. Finish the novel—complete the story, see it through, trust the narrative instead of abandoning it halfway when it gets difficult. All of it permission to imagine relationships could be different. Could be safe. Could handle his needs without breaking.

"I've been treating needing people as moral failure," he said quietly. "As proof I'm not actually capable of independence. But the reading said that's not true. Said wanting love and support isn't selfish."

"It's not," Erenne said. "It's human. You crave connection more than you admit. That's not weakness. That's reality."

"But every time I've shown that craving it's gone badly. My father dismissed it. Mira left. I learned that independence was safer than vulnerability."

"So you built façade. But façade isn't safety. Is loneliness pretending to be strength."

Sefiathan sat with that. Loneliness pretending to be strength. That landed accurate. He'd been lonely his whole life. Had craved deep connection, real support, relationships that could handle his actual needs. But he'd learned to call that craving selfishness. Learned to present as independent. Learned to treat his desire for love as shameful weakness instead of legitimate human need. And that hadn't made him safe. Had just made him isolated while pretending isolation was choice.

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this permission settling uncomfortably. It isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and immediately that voice rose—the one that said needing people was burden. That asking for support proved inadequacy. That vulnerability was weakness that gave others power to hurt him.

But the reading challenged that directly. The relationships worth having can handle your needs without breaking. Maybe every relationship he'd had so far couldn't handle his needs. But that didn't mean no relationship could. Didn't mean vulnerability always drove people away. Didn't mean wanting support was selfish. Meant he'd been surrounded by people who treated his humanity as burden. And that said something about them—not about whether his needs were legitimate.

The Don't list felt significant. Big break, Pirouettes, Hand mirror. Don't take break from connection just because it's hard. Don't perform independence like dance—spinning around looking graceful while actually just avoiding contact. Don't constantly check your reflection to make sure façade is still in place. The reading was giving permission to want love while warning against common traps. Against retreating into isolation when vulnerability got scary. Against treating independence as performance instead of recognizing legitimate craving for connection. Against maintaining façade instead of letting someone actually see him.

Afternoon brought moment that crystallized everything. He was sitting by the creek when Korrin approached—solid presence, steady friendship that had developed over months of traveling together. And Sefiathan felt it rising—genuine need for connection. Genuine desire to share what he'd been working through. Genuine craving to be seen and supported. And immediately he started building façade. Started preparing to present as fine, as handling everything alone, as not needing anything.

But he caught himself. Behind that independent façade, you crave connection more than you admit. This was it. This was the moment to practice. To let someone carry his weight for once. To stop treating vulnerability like weakness and trust that relationship worth having could handle his needs without breaking.

"I've been working through something hard," Sefiathan said, and his voice came out unsteady. "About how I learned that needing people was selfish. That showing vulnerability broke relationships. That I had to maintain independent façade to be acceptable."

Korrin sat down beside him without speaking. Steady presence. Not trying to fix anything. Just there.

"And I'm realizing that was lie," Sefiathan continued. "Was protection I built because every relationship I had taught me that my needs were burden. But the reading today said it isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. Said the relationships worth having can handle needs without breaking."

"They can," Korrin said simply.

"How do you know?"

"Because I'm here. Because you just told me something vulnerable and I'm still sitting next to you. Because relationship that can't handle your humanity isn't relationship worth having."

Sefiathan felt something break open in his chest. Not catastrophic breaking. Not the kind he'd feared his whole life. But breaking open of façade he'd been maintaining for decades. Korrin was right. He'd just shown vulnerability—had admitted he needed support, had dropped independent façade, had let someone see his craving for connection—and Korrin hadn't left. Hadn't dismissed him. Hadn't treated his need as burden. Had just stayed. Steady presence. Proof that relationships worth having could handle his needs without breaking.

They sat together by the creek in comfortable silence and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting. It isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. That wasn't just abstract permission. Was reality he could practice. Could test. Could trust. Because here was relationship that could handle his vulnerability. Here was person who didn't require him to maintain independent façade. Here was proof that wanting connection wasn't weakness—was human need that good relationships honored instead of punished.

Evening brought them back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And Sefiathan noticed something—how naturally he accepted help serving food. How easily he admitted he was tired. How comfortable he felt asking if they could eat outside because the evening was beautiful. Small vulnerabilities. Small needs. Small ways of letting people carry his weight for once instead of maintaining façade of complete independence.

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling September settling deeper. Twelve days into this month and the lessons kept building—honoring what happened, adapting instead of fighting, and now recognizing that wanting love and support wasn't selfish but human. All of it about dismantling façades he'd built to survive childhood that no longer served him. All of it about trusting that relationships worth having could handle his actual humanity.

It isn't selfish to want to be loved and supported. He felt those words settling like foundation. Like truth he could build on instead of shame he had to hide. He craved connection. He needed support. He wanted to be loved. And that wasn't weakness. Wasn't burden. Wasn't selfish. Was human. Was legitimate. Was exactly what relationships worth having were designed to hold. Tomorrow he'd keep practicing. Keep dropping façade. Keep letting people carry his weight. Keep trusting that vulnerability wasn't weakness but bridge to real connection. The independent façade had kept him safe but lonely. Now he could learn different way. Could crave connection without shame. Could need support without apology. Could want love without calling it selfish. Because it wasn't. Because he was human. Because relationships worth having could handle that. And he was learning—slowly, carefully, with practice—to let them.
