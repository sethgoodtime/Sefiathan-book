# **Day 315 — November 12, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his twelfth morning of November and the reading landed like an ending he'd been trying to postpone: It might be time to reevaluate. That relationship you've been preserving like a museum piece? It's rotting from within. The constant explanations you make for their behavior are just stories you tell yourself. Cut the cord. Reality awaits.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle with uncomfortable precision. Five days ago he'd felt reinvigorated, ready to take the first step forward, permission granted by his own body to move. And these past few days he'd been preparing without quite admitting what he was preparing for—gathering supplies, saying quiet goodbyes to the grasslands, letting his time at the waystation settle into memory instead of present tense. But this reading was pointing at something he'd been avoiding. Something about preservation. About museum pieces. About stories he told himself instead of facing reality.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling the prompt working through him. That relationship you've been preserving like a museum piece. What relationship? His first thought was his family—obviously. He'd been carrying them like museum pieces his whole life, preserving their disappointment in glass cases, making constant explanations for why they couldn't see him clearly. But he'd already done that work. Already recognized he was performing for ghosts. So what was the reading actually pointing at?

The waystation itself. His time here. The gentle stasis he'd been preserving for weeks now. The constant explanations he made for staying—I'm still learning, I'm doing slow work, I'm not quite ready—were just stories he told himself. The relationship with this place, with this pause in his journey, with the safety of not moving forward. That was what was rotting from within.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she was watching him with eyes that already knew.

"What's today's reading?"

"That it might be time to reevaluate. That the relationship I've been preserving like a museum piece is rotting from within. That the constant explanations I make are just stories I tell myself. That I need to cut the cord because reality awaits."

Erenne was quiet for a long moment, hands still on the teapot. Then she said, "You've been staying here too long."

The words landed gently but Sefiathan felt them like a punch. Not because they were cruel but because they were true. He'd been staying here too long. Not in days but in intention. He'd been preserving this place as safe harbor, making explanations for why he needed to keep learning and practicing and preparing instead of just admitting the truth—he was scared to move forward. Scared to actually live what he'd learned instead of sitting with it in comfortable stasis.

"I thought I was doing slow work," he said quietly. "Rewiring neural pathways. Taking 28 days to make new habits. But I've been here longer than that now and I'm still—staying. Making explanations for why it's not time yet."

"The constant explanations you make are just stories you tell yourself."

They ate together and Sefiathan felt something fundamental shifting. The reading was harsh but honest. It might be time to reevaluate. Not might—it was. He'd been preserving his time here like a museum piece, keeping it pristine and safe instead of letting it be what it needed to be—temporary. A waystation. Not a destination. The relationship with this place was rotting from within because he'd been trying to make it permanent when it was always meant to be passage.

The Do list felt pointed. Listening cap, Punchlines, Show not tell. Listening cap—actually hear what's being said instead of making excuses. Punchlines—let the ending land cleanly. Show not tell—actions speak, stop explaining. All of it pointing toward cutting the cord. Toward stopping the constant stories and facing reality. Reality awaits. Yes. He'd been hiding from it.

"I need to leave," he said, and the words felt both obvious and terrifying. "Not in a few days. Soon. Today or tomorrow. I've been making explanations for staying but they're just stories. The reality is I'm scared and I've been using preparation as an excuse to avoid moving forward."

"Reality awaits."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands knowing it might be one of his last times visiting this spot. He found his place by the creek and sat down, and instead of settling into comfortable contemplation he felt the reading pressing against everything he'd been preserving. That relationship you've been preserving like a museum piece. This whole month at the waystation had become a museum piece. Beautiful. Safe. Unchanging. But rotting from within because he was trying to keep it pristine instead of letting it be alive and temporary and real.

Cut the cord. The words felt violent but necessary. He'd been making constant explanations—I'm still learning, the slow work takes time, my body needs to be ready—but those were just stories. The truth was simpler and harder. He was ready. Had been ready for days. But cutting the cord meant facing reality and reality meant actually living differently instead of just recognizing how he needed to live differently.

The Don't list felt deliberate. Power through, Blinders on, Ruts. Don't force yourself forward with brute aggression. Don't refuse to see what you're avoiding. Don't stay in comfortable grooves just because they're familiar. The reading was asking him to face reality with clear eyes instead of powering through or hiding from what was actually happening.

He sat by the creek and let himself feel what reevaluation meant. It meant admitting he'd been preserving this pause in his journey because moving forward was terrifying. It meant recognizing the constant explanations he made for staying were just stories to protect himself from having to actually change. It meant cutting the cord with this safe harbor and trusting that reality—messy, uncertain, demanding reality—was what came next.

Afternoon brought Korrin to the creek and when he sat down Sefiathan was unusually still, feeling this ending settling through him.

"The reading said it might be time to reevaluate. That the relationship I've been preserving like a museum piece is rotting from within. That the constant explanations I make are just stories I tell myself. That I need to cut the cord because reality awaits."

Korrin was quiet for a long moment. Then he said, "You're leaving tomorrow."

Not a question. A recognition. Sefiathan felt his throat tighten but nodded. "Yes. I've been making explanations for why I needed to stay longer but they were just stories. The truth is I'm ready and I've been ready and I'm scared but that's not a reason to keep preserving this place as safe harbor."

"The constant explanations you make are just stories you tell yourself."

"I kept telling myself I was doing slow work, rewiring neural pathways, that 28 days meant I needed to stay through the end of November. But those were excuses. The slow work continues no matter where I am. Staying here has become about avoiding reality instead of preparing for it."

They sat together and Sefiathan felt the weight of this recognition settling. It might be time to reevaluate. He'd been reevaluating all morning and the answer was clear. Time to leave. Time to cut the cord. Time to stop preserving this beautiful pause and let it be what it was—temporary shelter that had done its work. The relationship with the waystation was rotting from within because he was trying to keep it instead of releasing it.

Evening brought them back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with clarity that felt both sad and necessary.

"I'm leaving tomorrow morning. I've been preserving this place like a museum piece, making constant explanations for why I needed to stay longer, but those were just stories. The reality is I'm ready and I'm scared and staying here is about avoiding that fear instead of facing it."

Erenne nodded slowly. "I know. I've known for days. I was waiting for you to know it too."

"The reading said to cut the cord. That reality awaits. It felt harsh but it was right. I've been hiding from reality by pretending I was still preparing when really I was just—pausing. Indefinitely."

Later in his room Sefiathan began packing his things with careful attention. Each item felt weighted with these weeks of stillness. His melody book. His worn boots. The small carved bird he'd made months ago in a clearing when his ankle was injured. The supplies Erenne had helped him gather. Everything fitting into his pack with familiar precision. Tomorrow morning he would leave. Tomorrow morning he would cut the cord with this safe harbor and face reality.

He lay in bed feeling November teaching him about reevaluation and museum pieces and stories told to avoid truth. About recognizing when preservation became rot. About constant explanations being just that—explanations, excuses, stories told to protect himself from having to actually live differently. About cutting the cord even when it felt violent. About reality awaiting—messy and uncertain and demanding but real in ways that museum pieces could never be.

Tomorrow he would leave. Would take the first step that his body had been ready for days ago. Would stop preserving this pause and let it become memory. Would face reality instead of hiding from it. The journey continuing. Not because he had perfect clarity or complete transformation or all his neural pathways rewired. But because staying here any longer would mean rotting from within. Would mean choosing stories over truth. Would mean preserving safety instead of choosing aliveness. Reality awaits. Tomorrow he would meet it.
