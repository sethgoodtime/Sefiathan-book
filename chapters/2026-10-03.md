# **Day 275 — October 3, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his third morning of October and the reading landed like his body staging an intervention: You're due for an oil change. Run yourself a hot bath. You've been grinding so hard that you've forgotten what it feels like to actually rest. Your body is sending signals you keep ignoring. Tonight, draw that bath. Tomorrow can wait.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle with uncomfortable accuracy. Three days ago September had ended with permission to feel broken hearts breaking, to stop logic-ing his way out of missing people, to let grief be grief instead of problem requiring analysis. And he'd been carrying that—sitting with the hurt, letting himself miss his mother and Darius and Mira without strategic distance, recognizing that all broken hearts eventually heal but not through timeline or analysis. Had been learning to feel bad now so he could feel better later.

But he'd also been grinding. Moving forward relentlessly. Taking each day's lesson and applying it immediately, pushing himself to grow faster, to understand deeper, to transform more completely. Treating his journey like project with deadline instead of experience with rhythm. And his body was tired. Actually tired. Sending signals he'd been ignoring because tomorrow always seemed more important than rest.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling how much his body wanted to stay horizontal. You've been grinding so hard that you've forgotten what it feels like to actually rest. When was the last time he'd genuinely rested? Not just stopped moving but actually let his body recover? He'd been treating rest like weakness. Like wasted time. Like thing he'd do once he'd made enough progress to deserve it. But the reading said otherwise. You're due for an oil change. His body wasn't machine that could run indefinitely on determination. Was living thing that needed care. Needed rest. Needed him to actually listen to signals he kept ignoring.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she took one look at him and nodded.

"You look exhausted."

"The reading said I'm due for an oil change. That I've been grinding so hard I've forgotten what it feels like to actually rest. That my body is sending signals I keep ignoring."

Erenne poured tea and sat down across from him. "And you've been treating rest like something you have to earn."

"Yes. Like if I just push a little harder, make a little more progress, understand a little deeper—then I'll deserve to stop. But my body is tired. Actually tired. And I keep ignoring that because tomorrow always seems more important than recovery."

"But you can't actually make progress when you're running on empty. You're just grinding yourself down. Wearing yourself out. Forgetting that rest isn't weakness—it's requirement."

Sefiathan felt something shift in how he was holding his tiredness. She was right. He'd been treating his body like obstacle to push through rather than partner that needed care. Ignoring signals because he thought powering through was strength. But grinding yourself into exhaustion wasn't strength. Was just grinding. And eventually the machine broke down. The reading was telling him he was at that point. Due for oil change. Due for actual rest. Due to stop ignoring what his body had been trying to tell him for days now—slow down, recover, let tomorrow wait.

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. PDAs, Fast and loose, Staring contest. Permission to show affection for his own body instead of treating it like enemy. Fast and loose—let his muscles actually relax instead of staying tensed for next task. Staring contest—look directly at his exhaustion instead of avoiding it, acknowledge what his body was actually experiencing. All of it pointing toward same thing. Draw that bath. Let yourself rest. Stop grinding long enough to recover.

"I think I've been afraid that if I stop pushing I'll lose momentum," he said quietly. "Like rest means giving up. Like my body being tired means I'm not trying hard enough."

"But you can't sustain momentum when you're exhausted. You just burn out. And your body being tired doesn't mean you're not trying hard enough—means you've been trying too hard without recovery. Means you need oil change before the engine seizes."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan stayed at the waystation instead of walking out to the grasslands. That itself felt strange—choosing to stay, to not push himself toward next lesson or insight or moment of growth. Just staying. Letting his body have what it needed. He found quiet corner and sat down, and immediately his mind started generating guilt. He should be doing something productive. Should be working on growth. Should be making progress toward whatever tomorrow held. Sitting here resting was wasting time.

But he caught himself. The reading wasn't suggesting rest as luxury. Was naming it as requirement. You're due for an oil change. Not "you might consider rest if you have time" but "your body needs this and you keep ignoring that need." The guilt about resting wasn't wisdom—was the grinding voice that had forgotten rest was part of the work, not obstacle to it.

The Don't list felt pointed. Wait by the phone, Thumb twiddling, Divided attention. Don't put off rest while waiting for perfect moment that never comes. Don't treat recovery like meaningless waste of time. Don't try to rest while simultaneously doing seventeen other things. The reading was giving permission to actually rest while warning against common avoidance patterns. Against postponing recovery. Against devaluing stillness. Against half-measures that prevented genuine restoration.

Afternoon brought Korrin to the waystation and when he found Sefiathan still sitting in quiet corner, his expression shifted to concern.

"Are you alright?"

"I'm tired. Actually tired. The reading said I'm due for an oil change. That I've been grinding so hard I've forgotten what rest feels like. That my body is sending signals I keep ignoring."

Korrin sat down carefully beside him. "You have been pushing very hard. Every day a new lesson. Every night processing what you learned. Never stopping long enough to actually recover."

"I thought that was strength. Thought pushing through exhaustion meant I was committed. But I think I've just been wearing myself out. Treating my body like obstacle instead of partner. Ignoring what it needs because tomorrow always seemed more important."

"But you can't actually meet tomorrow well if you arrive there exhausted. Sometimes the strongest thing you can do is stop. Let yourself recover. Draw the bath the reading is telling you to draw."

They sat together and Sefiathan felt permission settling through him like unfamiliar weight. Draw that bath. When was the last time he'd done something purely for his body's comfort? Not as reward for progress but as basic care? He'd been so focused on emotional growth, on understanding lessons, on transforming his relationship with himself—he'd forgotten his body was part of that self. Was carrying him through this journey. Was sending signals he kept ignoring because he thought pushing through was more important than listening.

Evening brought dinner where Erenne had prepared extra comfort food—warm bread, thick stew, honey tea. And when Sefiathan looked at her questioningly she smiled.

"You need oil change. Thought I'd start with the fuel."

After dinner he went upstairs and actually drew himself a bath. Hot water. Herbs Erenne had given him that smelled like meadow grass and summer rain. And when he lowered himself into the water he felt his body respond with relief so profound it almost hurt. This was what he'd been ignoring. This need for actual rest. For care. For stopping long enough to let exhausted muscles relax and tired mind quiet and grinding momentum finally cease.

He stayed in the bath until the water cooled. Let himself be still. Let tomorrow wait like the reading had told him to. And when he finally got out and dried off and climbed into bed, his body felt different. Not recovered completely—oil changes took time. But acknowledged. Cared for. Given what it needed instead of being ignored and pushed through.

Tomorrow he would keep learning. Keep growing. Keep moving through his journey toward whatever December held. But tonight he was just resting. Actually resting. Letting his body have the recovery it had been asking for. Recognizing that grinding himself to exhaustion wasn't strength or commitment or dedication—was just grinding. And eventually the machine broke down if you never changed the oil.

You're due for an oil change. He was. Had been for a while. And finally, after months of ignoring his body's signals, after pushing through tiredness like it was obstacle rather than information—finally he was listening. Drawing the bath. Letting tomorrow wait. Giving himself permission to stop grinding long enough to recover. Because rest wasn't weakness. Wasn't wasted time. Wasn't something he had to earn through sufficient progress. Was requirement. Was care. Was what bodies needed when they'd been carrying you through transformative journey without complaint while you ignored every signal they sent.

Tonight he would sleep deeply. Tomorrow he would wake still tired but moving toward recovered. And he would remember this—that his body was partner in this journey, not obstacle. That signals about exhaustion were information worth listening to, not weakness worth ignoring. That sometimes the strongest thing you could do was stop. Rest. Change the oil before the engine seized. Let yourself be cared for instead of endlessly pushing toward tomorrow that would still be there after you'd recovered enough to actually meet it well.
