# **Day 272 — September 30, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on September's final morning and the reading landed like permission to stop performing strength: All broken hearts eventually. Stop trying to logic your way out of missing them. The heart doesn't operate on your timeline or respond to your analysis. Feel bad now so you can feel better later.

He lay in bed feeling those words settle with weight of recognition that made something crack open in his chest. Yesterday the reading had told him there was a time for everything, that his brilliance lay in seeing ten steps ahead but life unfolded in single footsteps, that he should match his pace to reality instead of forcing reality to match his pace. And he'd been practicing that—recognizing that seeing the mountain didn't obligate him to sprint there, learning to be present to single footsteps instead of rushing toward outcomes he could already map, trusting that the mountain wasn't going anywhere and neither was he. Had been learning to let life unfold at its own pace even when his intelligence could see where things were heading.

But underneath that practice had been careful avoidance of one particular territory. Missing people. He'd been logic-ing his way around it. Analyzing why he missed them, when the missing would fade, what it meant about his growth or lack thereof. Treating heartbreak like problem to solve instead of experience to feel. The reading cut through all that strategic distance. All broken hearts eventually. The heart doesn't operate on your timeline or respond to your analysis. Feel bad now so you can feel better later.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan sat up slowly, feeling that permission working through him like uncomfortable relief. He missed people. Missed his mother's garden conversations even though their relationship was complicated. Missed Darius from Greymark even though that friendship had ended badly. Missed Mira even though she'd moved on to marry his father's other apprentice. Missed versions of people who maybe never existed outside his perception but felt real enough in memory. And he'd been trying to logic his way out of all that missing. Analyzing it into something manageable. Putting it on timeline where it would eventually resolve. Treating his heart like it should respond to rational analysis instead of just feeling what it felt.

But the reading said otherwise. The heart doesn't operate on your timeline. All those careful strategic approaches to missing people—they weren't actually helping. Were just ways to avoid feeling bad now. But feeling bad now was how you felt better later. Not through analysis. Not through timeline. Not through logic. Through actually letting the broken heart be broken.

Downstairs Erenne was making breakfast and when Sefiathan entered the kitchen she looked at him and her expression softened.

"That's the face of someone who just got told to stop avoiding their feelings."

"The reading said all broken hearts eventually. That I should stop trying to logic my way out of missing people. That the heart doesn't operate on my timeline or respond to my analysis. That I need to feel bad now so I can feel better later."

Erenne poured tea and sat down across from him. "And you've been analyzing the missing instead of feeling it."

"Yes. Trying to understand why I miss people. When it will fade. What it means. Treating heartbreak like problem with solution instead of just—" He stopped, feeling something tighten in his throat. "Instead of just missing them."

"But missing them hurts. And you're very good at finding ways to avoid hurt through strategic thinking."

Sefiathan felt that observation land accurate and uncomfortable. He was good at that. Could analyze himself out of almost any feeling. Could put emotions on timeline, map their trajectory, logic his way around actually experiencing them. But the heart didn't operate that way. Didn't care about his strategic distance. Didn't respond to analysis. Just felt what it felt. And all the logic in the world wouldn't make the missing go away. Would just postpone actually feeling it.

They ate together and Sefiathan felt the Do list working through him. Endings, Bad poetry, Bonfires. Permission to acknowledge what's finished, to express feeling badly even when it comes out messy, to gather around warmth of shared experience. Endings—stop pretending things aren't over. Bad poetry—let the grief come out however it comes out, even if it's not elegant or analyzed. Bonfires—sit with others around fire, let feeling be communal rather than isolated strategic project. All of it pointing toward same thing. Feel bad now so you can feel better later.

"I've been treating missing people like weakness," he said quietly. "Like if I was growing properly I wouldn't still miss them. Wouldn't still have broken heart about endings that happened months ago. Would have logic'd my way past it by now."

"But broken hearts don't heal on your timeline. And missing people isn't weakness. It's just what hearts do when they've loved and lost."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands feeling this permission settling through him like dam about to break. Stop trying to logic your way out of missing them. What did that mean practically? He found his spot by the creek and sat down, and immediately his mind started generating analysis. Why he missed them. What that said about his growth. When he'd stop feeling this way. How to resolve it faster. All the strategic approaches to heartbreak he'd been using to avoid actually feeling it.

But he caught himself. The reading wasn't offering solution. Was offering permission. All broken hearts eventually. Everyone's heart broke. Everyone missed people. Everyone had endings that hurt long after they were over. That wasn't unique to him. Wasn't sign of failure. Wasn't problem needing solving. Was just how hearts worked. They broke. They missed. They hurt. And the way through wasn't analysis. Was feeling bad now so you could feel better later.

The Don't list felt pointed. Sit by the phone, Cry wolf, Interpret silence. Don't wait for people who aren't coming back. Don't manufacture drama to avoid genuine feeling. Don't analyze what someone's silence means instead of just sitting with how their absence feels. The reading was giving permission to feel while warning against common avoidance strategies. Against waiting instead of grieving. Against false emergencies instead of real hurt. Against intellectual interpretation instead of emotional experience.

Afternoon brought moment that broke something open. He was sitting by the creek thinking about his mother and suddenly he wasn't analyzing why he missed her or when that would fade. Was just missing her. Missing the way she spoke to plants. Missing her hands in garden soil. Missing the version of her who existed before their relationship got so complicated. And it hurt. Actually hurt. Felt like something breaking in his chest. But underneath the hurt was strange relief. Because he'd been carrying this missing around in strategic distance for months. Analyzing it. Timeline-ing it. Logic-ing around it. And none of that had made it go away. Had just made him exhausted from avoiding feeling.

Now he was just feeling it. Missing his mother. And it was bad. But it was also real. Was his heart doing what hearts do. Breaking. Missing. Hurting. Not on his timeline. Not responding to his analysis. Just feeling what it felt.

When Korrin found him by the creek later that afternoon, Sefiathan's face was wet and his chest hurt and he wasn't analyzing any of it.

"You're crying," Korrin said carefully.

"The reading said I should stop trying to logic my way out of missing people. That all broken hearts eventually. That I need to feel bad now so I can feel better later."

"And you've been trying to analyze your way past the hurt."

"Yes. But it doesn't work. The heart doesn't operate on my timeline. Doesn't respond to analysis. Just feels what it feels."

Korrin sat down beside him. "Who are you missing?"

"My mother. Darius. Mira. Versions of people who maybe never existed. Endings that are months old but still hurt. I keep thinking I should be past it by now. Should have logic'd my way through. But the missing doesn't care about my strategic timeline."

They sat together and Sefiathan felt something fundamental in how he was holding this. Not analyzing. Not timeline-ing. Not trying to logic his way out. Just sitting with broken heart that was doing what broken hearts do. Missing people. Hurting about endings. Feeling bad now so he could eventually feel better later. Not because he'd analyzed it into resolution. But because he'd actually let himself feel it.

Evening brought them back to the waystation where Erenne had dinner ready. And when she asked about his day, Sefiathan answered with less strategic distance than usual.

"I cried by the creek. Let myself miss people without analyzing why or when it would fade. Just felt the broken heart being broken."

"How was that?"

"Bad. But also—real. I've been carrying missing around in strategic distance for months. Today I just let it hurt."

Later in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling September ending. Thirty days of learning. About observing patterns. About recognizing genius. About extending himself patience. About matching pace to reality. And now about letting broken hearts break. About feeling bad now so he could feel better later. About stopping the endless analysis and just sitting with what his heart was actually feeling.

All broken hearts eventually. Tomorrow would be October. New month. New lessons. But tonight he would just be here. Feeling what he felt. Missing who he missed. Letting his heart operate on its own timeline instead of forcing it to match his analysis. The missing would fade eventually. The broken heart would heal eventually. But not through logic. Not through strategic distance. Not through timeline-ing or analyzing or avoiding. Through feeling bad now. Through sitting with hurt. Through letting heart do what hearts do when they've loved and lost. Break. Miss. Hurt. And eventually, in their own time, heal. Not on his schedule. Not through his analysis. Just eventually. All broken hearts eventually.
