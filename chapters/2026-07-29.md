# **Day 209 — July 29, 2026**

Sefiathan woke at the waystation to Erenne already moving in the kitchen, bread baking, morning light soft through windows. He'd slept deeply for the first time in days and the rest felt almost suspicious—like his body had been waiting for permission to actually recover instead of just continuing to function while he pushed through exhaustion. Three days here now. Three days of staying put after those five lonely days of walking. Three days of not punishing himself for needing to stop.

The reading's truth hit before he was fully awake: It matters who you spend your nights with. Not romantic. Not prescriptive. Just recognition that the people you're around during vulnerable hours—when you're tired, when defenses are down, when you're just existing without performance—those people shape you differently than casual daytime encounters.

He thought about the past months. The excavation scholars who'd let him hide in useful work. Korrin and Tavis whose companionship had felt easy but ultimately temporary. Thessaly who'd called him oddball and meant it as recognition. Calantha who'd taught him practical magic without demanding he perform gratitude. And now Erenne, who'd simply offered tea and space to process without trying to fix anything.

Each morning-after leaves you emptier than before. The reading's expansion made him sit up in bed. Sefiathan had been treating connection like casual consumption. Spending nights—literal and metaphorical—with people who felt safe and comfortable but didn't actually nourish anything deeper. The excavation camp had been warm bodies and pleasant conversation but he'd left feeling emptier, recognizing he'd been hiding. Even before that, how many stops had he made where the company felt good in the moment but left nothing lasting?

Your body deserves the same careful standards you apply to everything else. He'd been meticulous about his journey in some ways—tracking his magic development, noticing patterns of self-punishment, recognizing when he was lying to himself. But careless about who he spent vulnerable time with. Careless about whether those connections actually fed something or just provided temporary warmth. Stop settling for warmth without substance.

Erenne found him sitting on the waystation porch watching grasslands turn gold in morning sun. "You're thinking loud this morning."

"The reading said it matters who you spend your nights with. That casual encounters leave you emptier. That I should stop settling for warmth without substance." He paused, feeling the truth settle. "I've been careless about who I'm around when I'm vulnerable. Just grateful for any company after being alone. Not actually paying attention to whether the connection nourishes anything or just provides temporary comfort."

She sat beside him with her own tea. "And am I warmth without substance?"

The question was direct but not accusatory. Sefiathan considered it honestly. "No. You let me process without fixing. You saw me struggling and didn't make it mean I was broken. That's different than just pleasant company."

"What about the others? The scholars at the excavation?"

"Warmth without substance. Good people but I was hiding in their project. Using their company to avoid being alone with myself. Left feeling emptier because I'd spent two months with them and still didn't know what I actually wanted."

The grasslands stretched endless and he felt the pattern clearly now. How many stops had he made where the company felt good but left nothing real? Where he'd been grateful for any connection that made him feel less alone but hadn't actually chosen people who nourished something deeper? He'd been settling. Taking whatever warmth was available instead of being careful about whose presence he allowed during vulnerable hours.

The Do list felt pointed. Spice. FwB. Airhugs. All things that could feel nourishing or empty depending on intention. Spice could enhance flavor or just mask bland food. Friends with benefits could be honest connection or just settling for physical warmth without actual intimacy. Air hugs—the performance of closeness without real contact. He'd been doing all of that. Performing connection. Settling for surface warmth. Not actually choosing carefully who got access to his vulnerable hours.

A traveler arrived midmorning—young woman leading a tired horse, clearly pushing too hard. Erenne offered water and rest but the woman was in a hurry, just needed to water the horse and keep moving. Sefiathan watched the interaction and saw himself. That restless pushing. That refusal to actually stop and nourish. That settling for quick warmth—a brief conversation, a moment of human contact—instead of choosing to stay long enough for real substance.

"She'll be back," Erenne said after the woman left. "They always are. Can't sustain that pace. Can't keep taking quick warmth instead of real nourishment."

Sefiathan felt that land. How many times had he done exactly that? Stopped just long enough to feel less alone, then pushed forward before any real connection could form. Settling for brief encounters that left him emptier instead of choosing to stay with people who actually fed something deeper.

The Don't list made sense now. Coyness. Fake niceties. Broken plans. All the ways he performed connection instead of choosing it carefully. The coyness that avoided direct honesty about what he needed from people. The fake niceties that substituted for real intimacy. The broken plans that let him avoid commitment to deeper connection by keeping everything casual and temporary.

Afternoon heat built and Sefiathan helped Erenne in the garden, hands in soil, working quietly together. This felt different than the excavation work. Felt nourishing instead of just useful. They weren't performing companionship or filling time. Just existing together, working, occasionally talking but mostly quiet. His body recognized the difference. Recognized that vulnerable time spent with someone who saw him clearly felt fundamentally different than casual encounters that just provided warmth.

"How do you know?" he asked eventually. "How do you know if someone's worth spending vulnerable time with?"

Erenne considered while tying up tomato plants. "Ask yourself if you leave feeling fuller or emptier. If you have to perform or if you can just exist. If they see you or if they see their idea of you. If being around them nourishes something deeper or just provides temporary comfort."

Simple metrics but Sefiathan felt their truth. The excavation scholars—he'd had to perform usefulness. Left feeling emptier. They'd seen their idea of him as helpful strange magic user, not who he actually was. Temporary comfort that masked deeper emptiness.

Calantha had been different. Thessaly too. Korrin. They'd seen him. Let him exist without performance. Left him feeling fuller, even when the encounters were brief. That was the distinction. Not about duration. About whether the connection actually nourished something or just provided warmth without substance.

That evening three more travelers arrived—a family heading east toward the coast. Erenne offered them food and beds and Sefiathan watched how she interacted with them. Warm but boundaried. Present but not performing. She gave them what they needed without depleting herself. Knew the difference between nourishing hospitality and empty warmth.

Over dinner the father asked about Sefiathan's journey and he felt the familiar pull to perform it. To make it sound purposeful and certain and like he knew what he was doing. But Erenne's presence steadied him. Let him just be honest instead. "I'm learning to stop settling for casual connection. To be more careful about who I spend vulnerable time with."

The father looked confused but the mother nodded like she understood completely. "That's harder than it sounds. Everyone wants company when they're tired. Takes real discipline to choose carefully instead of just taking whatever warmth is available."

After the family settled for the night, Sefiathan sat with Erenne on the porch watching stars appear. "I need to be more careful going forward. About who I stop with. Who I let in during vulnerable hours. I've been settling for any company instead of choosing people who actually nourish something."

"You planning to leave soon?"

"Maybe. But not because staying is settling. Because I'm ready. There's a difference."

She smiled. "There is. And knowing that difference matters."

Sefiathan lay in bed that night feeling the shift settling. It mattered who you spent your nights with. Not just romantic nights. Vulnerable nights. Tired nights. Nights when you were just existing without armor. Those encounters shaped you. Fed you or emptied you. And he'd been careless about them. Grateful for any connection instead of choosing carefully who got access to his undefended hours.

Stop settling for warmth without substance. Simple instruction but revolutionary practice. He could be alone if the alternative was casual encounters that left him emptier. Could choose carefully instead of desperately. Could recognize that his body—his vulnerable self—deserved the same careful standards he'd started applying to everything else. Deserved connections that actually nourished instead of just temporarily masked loneliness.

The waystation was quiet around him and Sefiathan felt genuinely grateful for Erenne's presence. Not just warmth. Actual substance. Someone who'd let him process and rest and exist without performance. Someone whose company left him fuller instead of emptier. That was worth staying for. Worth choosing carefully. Worth recognizing as fundamentally different than the casual encounters he'd been settling for.

Tomorrow he might leave or he might stay longer. But either way, going forward, he'd be more careful about who he spent his vulnerable hours with. More honest about whether connection nourished or just provided temporary comfort. More willing to be alone than to settle for warmth without substance. His body deserved that. His journey deserved that. He deserved that.
