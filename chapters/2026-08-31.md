# **Day 242 — August 31, 2026**

Sefiathan woke on his thirty-first morning at the waystation and the reading hit him like cold water: You won't be happy with someone who doesn't know how to take care of themselves. That person makes you feel needed—their chaos gives you purpose. But notice the exhaustion creeping in. Their problems never end because they don't want solutions. You're not their savior; you're their crutch. Time to want better.

He lay very still feeling those words land in places he'd been carefully avoiding. Not about romantic connection—he wasn't carrying that particular burden. But about something deeper and more pervasive. About the way he'd been showing up for people his whole life. About the pattern of gravitating toward those who needed him, who made him feel useful, who gave his intensity somewhere to go. About how he'd built his entire sense of worth around being the person others leaned on when they couldn't stand on their own.

Morning light filtered through his window and Sefiathan felt the exhaustion the reading named. Real exhaustion. Not from the waystation work or the gardening or the simple rhythms he'd built here. Exhaustion from twenty-seven years of being crutch instead of companion. Of seeking out people whose chaos gave him purpose instead of people who had their own foundation. Of believing his value came from fixing or helping or carrying others instead of from simply being himself.

He thought about his family. His mother who'd leaned on him emotionally in ways she'd never asked of Elara. His father whose disappointment had kept Sefiathan constantly trying to prove himself useful. The dynamic where he'd become the one who absorbed everyone else's feelings, who made space for their chaos, who gave them somewhere to put their problems without ever expecting solutions. He'd felt needed. Had felt like he had purpose. But the reading was naming it plainly: their problems never end because they don't want solutions. You're not their savior; you're their crutch.

Breakfast smells drifted from the kitchen and Sefiathan got dressed slowly, feeling this recognition settling uncomfortably. He'd been doing the same thing with strangers. Yesterday with the traveling family—the automatic pull to help with the baby not from genuine desire but from pattern. From needing to be needed. From seeking validation through usefulness instead of trusting his aloneness was enough. He'd said no yesterday, had honored his boundaries, but now he saw the deeper pattern. Saw how often he gravitated toward those who didn't know how to take care of themselves because their need made him feel valuable.

Downstairs Erenne was making eggs and looked up when he entered, reading something in his face immediately.

"Heavy morning," she said simply.

"The reading said I won't be happy with someone who doesn't know how to take care of themselves. Said I've been gravitating toward people whose chaos gives me purpose. Said their problems never end because they don't want solutions and I'm not their savior, I'm their crutch."

Erenne set down her spatula and looked at him directly. "And you're seeing the pattern."

"Everywhere. My whole life. My mother leaning on me emotionally. My father whose disappointment kept me proving myself useful. Even here—the way I automatically move toward helping people not from genuine desire but from needing to be needed. I've built my entire sense of worth around being someone's crutch."

They sat together with tea and Sefiathan felt the reading's words working through him like medicine that burned. The exhaustion was real. Years of it. Decades of absorbing other people's chaos and calling it connection. Of seeking out those who needed saving and feeling valuable when they leaned on him. Of never asking whether they actually wanted solutions or just wanted endless place to put their problems. Of playing savior when what was really happening was he needed to be needed more than they needed to change.

"What does wanting better look like?" Erenne asked quietly.

Sefiathan sat with that question feeling something fundamental shifting. "It means gravitating toward people who already know how to take care of themselves. Means connection based on genuine wanting instead of desperate needing. Means my value doesn't come from being useful but from being myself. Means trusting my aloneness enough that I don't need someone's chaos to give me purpose."

"That's scary."

"Terrifying. Because if I'm not fixing or helping or carrying someone, who am I? If I'm not needed, am I worth anything?"

"That's the question, isn't it."

Morning stretched into midday and Sefiathan walked out into the grasslands alone, needing movement to process this. The pattern was everywhere once he saw it clearly. Mira back in Millhaven—his father's apprentice who'd looked to him for validation and reassurance. He'd felt needed there. Felt valuable. But had she actually wanted him or had she wanted crutch? Had their connection been genuine or had it been built on her chaos giving him purpose?

Even with Korrin and Tavis—he'd been drawn to helping rescue Tavis in the storm. Had felt useful in that moment. But that had been different, he realized. That had been genuine crisis requiring genuine help. Not endless chaos that never resolved. Not pattern of someone who didn't want solutions, just wanted somewhere to put their problems. Korrin and Tavis took care of themselves. Were complete on their own. Their occasional need for help wasn't their identity.

The difference mattered. The reading wasn't saying never help anyone. Was saying notice who gravitates toward you because you make them feel safe in their chaos versus who shows up already knowing how to take care of themselves and occasionally needs genuine support. Was saying notice the exhaustion that comes from being crutch instead of companion. Was saying time to want better.

Afternoon heat built and Sefiathan sat in tall grass feeling the month ending tomorrow. August almost finished. Thirty-one days at this waystation learning things he'd been avoiding his whole life. Learning he was incredible. Learning to trust his aloneness. Learning to stop being everyone's savior. The lessons kept building on each other, kept cracking open patterns he'd carried for decades.

A merchant caravan passed on the road and Sefiathan watched them go, thinking about Thessaly's letter. Wondering if she'd received it yet. Wondering what her response would be if she chose to write back. But even that wondering felt different now. He'd written the letter from genuine desire for connection, not from needing to be needed. Had been honest about his strangeness without apologizing. Had trusted his aloneness enough that her response wasn't make-or-break. If she wrote back, good. If not, he'd be fine. He didn't need her chaos to give him purpose. Didn't need to be her savior. Could just be himself and trust that was enough.

Evening brought him back to the waystation where Erenne was preparing dinner. He helped in comfortable silence and felt the shift settling deeper. Tomorrow was September first. New month. New chapter. The reading's final instruction felt critical now: Time to want better. Not just recognize the pattern. Not just see the exhaustion. Actually want different. Actually choose connection based on mutual wholeness instead of desperate need.

The traveling family was packing to leave in the morning and the mother stopped him in the hallway. "Thank you for your help with the garden," she said warmly. "My daughter loved watching you work with the plants."

Sefiathan smiled but felt the difference in how he received it. Not desperate gratitude that he'd been useful. Not validation that he had value because someone needed him. Just simple appreciation for genuine sharing. "She has good instincts," he said. "Natural curiosity about how things grow."

The mother nodded and moved on and Sefiathan felt that small interaction settle. No rescuing. No being crutch. Just honest exchange between two people who took care of themselves and could share genuine moment without either needing the other to be savior.

Later alone in his room Sefiathan lay in bed feeling the weight of this final August lesson. He'd spent his whole life gravitating toward those who didn't know how to take care of themselves because their chaos gave him purpose. Had believed his value came from being needed. Had exhausted himself being everyone's crutch while calling it connection. But that wasn't love. That was transaction. That was him needing to be needed more than them actually wanting solutions.

Time to want better. Time to trust his aloneness enough that he didn't need someone's chaos to give him purpose. Time to believe his value came from being himself, not from fixing others. Time to gravitate toward people who already took care of themselves and could show up whole instead of desperately needing him to hold them together. The right people wouldn't need him to be their savior. Would just want him to be himself. And that wanting—that genuine choice for connection between two complete people—that would be worth having. That would be real.

Tomorrow was September. Tomorrow was new chapter. Tomorrow he'd keep practicing wanting better. Keep trusting he was enough without needing to be anyone's crutch. Keep believing connection built on mutual wholeness instead of desperate need was actually possible. The exhaustion was real. The pattern was clear. Time to choose different. Time to want better. Time to let being himself be enough.
